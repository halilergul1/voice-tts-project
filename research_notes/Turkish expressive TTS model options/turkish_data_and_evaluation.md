# Turkish TTS: public speech datasets and evaluation practice (as of 2026-10-26)

Scope note: this covers (1) publicly available Turkish speech corpora usable for TTS (with emphasis on emotional / acted / audiobook / podcast / broadcast material), (2) emotion-labelled Turkish data, (3) legal/licence context for broadcast-derived training data in Turkey (factual, not legal advice), (4) how multilingual/Turkish TTS is evaluated and which Turkish ASR models are used for scoring, and (5) Turkish text-frontend pitfalls and tools. "Verified" = taken from a dataset card / paper read directly; "search-summary" = taken from a search-engine summary of a page I could not open (flagged inline).

## Key Question 1: Catalogue of Turkish speech corpora (hours, speakers, sample rate, conditions, transcripts, licence, TTS vs ASR suitability)

### Takeaway
As of October 2026 the largest *openly downloadable* Turkish speech pools are web-scraped (YODAS tr ≈ 4.7k h, Hugging Face audiobook scrapes ≈ 2.7k–3.1k h, podcast scrapes ≈ 115k segments) with machine transcripts and uncleared copyright, while the *clean, licence-safe* pools are small (Common Voice 25.0 ≈ 129 validated h CC0; FLEURS ≈ 12 h; ISSAI TSC 218 h with a licence conflict). There is no Turkish equivalent of LibriTTS/Expresso; the Turkish-first TTS papers of 2026 (FreyaTTS, EMA-TTS) all train on undisclosed "internal" corpora.

### Cited Findings

**Crowd-sourced read speech (clean, permissive)**
- Common Voice Scripted Speech 25.0 – Turkish, released 22 March 2026: 126,510 clips, 135.29 h recorded, 129.22 h validated, 1,816 speakers, from a text corpus of 413,900 sentences (search-summary of the Mozilla Data Collective datasheet; the page is JS-rendered and did not load in a fetch) — [Mozilla Data Collective, CV Scripted 25.0 Turkish](https://datacollective.mozillafoundation.org/datasets/cmn2e7kbl01k2mm07gm5n1bc9)
- Prior release 24.0 Turkish: 134.19 h recorded, 128.58 h validated, 1,795 speakers (search-summary) — [CV Scripted 24.0 Turkish](https://datacollective.mozillafoundation.org/datasets/cmj8u3px500s1nxxb4qh79iqr)
- Common Voice *Spontaneous* Speech 3.0 – Turkish (22 March 2026) is negligible: 46 clips, 0.27 h recorded, 0.14 h validated, 10 speakers (search-summary) — [CV Spontaneous 3.0 Turkish](https://prod.datacollective.mozillafoundation.org/datasets/cmn1pleap00tho107nvvwnbyj)
- Common Voice is no longer distributed through Hugging Face: the `mozilla-foundation/common_voice_17_0` card is now empty (2.88 kB) and states that "Effective October 2025" Common Voice datasets are "exclusively available through Mozilla Data Collective" — [HF common_voice_17_0](https://huggingface.co/datasets/mozilla-foundation/common_voice_17_0)
- A speech-restored (Sidon) derivative of Common Voice 22 covering 137 languages is on HF under CC0, tagged text-to-speech — [sarulab-speech/commonvoice22_sidon](https://huggingface.co/datasets/sarulab-speech/commonvoice22_sidon)
- FLEURS: each sentence is recorded by 3 native speakers, ≈12 h per language, 1.4k h total; `tr_tr` (Turkish, Turkey) is one of the languages; most languages have 2,400–3,300 train utterances averaging ≈12 s — [FLEURS paper, arXiv 2205.12446](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.12446)
- MediaSpeech: 10 h per language for ar, fr, es, tr (tagged ASR + TTS) — [ymoslem/MediaSpeech](https://huggingface.co/datasets/ymoslem/MediaSpeech)

**Academic / institutional corpora**
- ISSAI Turkish Speech Corpus (TSC): 218.2 h, 186,171 utterances; HF card licence tag = MIT; speakers, sample rate, sources and splits are NOT stated on the card; citation Mussakhojayeva et al. 2023, *Information* 14(2):74 — [issai/Turkish_Speech_Corpus](https://huggingface.co/datasets/issai/Turkish_Speech_Corpus); the ISSAI site lists the same corpus under CC BY 4.0 (licence conflict) — [ISSAI Datasets](https://issai.nu.edu.kz/?p=12859)
- A community derivative filters TSC to 94 h and adds speaker diarization, tagged for TTS — [ulaspolat/tsc-tr-filtered-94h](https://huggingface.co/datasets/ulaspolat/tsc-tr-filtered-94h)
- LDC2012S06 "Turkish Broadcast News Speech and Transcripts" (Boğaziçi University): ≈130 h of Voice of America Turkish radio collected Dec 2006–Jun 2009 (2006–08 from analog FM, 2009 from satellite), released 16 May 2012; LDC licence (paid, research) — [LDC2012S06](https://catalog.ldc.upenn.edu/LDC2012S06)
- LDC2006S33 "METU Turkish Microphone Speech v1.0": ≈5.6 h, 120 METU students/faculty/staff each reading 40 sentences, released 18 May 2006 — [LDC2006S33](https://catalog.ldc.upenn.edu/LDC2006S33)
- IARPA Babel Turkish language pack (telephone conversational speech; ASR-oriented) is also on LDC — [LDC2016S10](https://catalog.ldc.upenn.edu/LDC2016S10)
- "Turkish Continuous and Isolated Word Speech Database" is registered on ISLRN — [ISLRN 192-049-804-522-1](https://www.islrn.org/resources/192-049-804-522-1)
- Commercial vendor catalogues list Turkish ASR corpora (e.g. Appen tur-asr002) — [Appen data catalog](https://www.appen.com/data-catalog/audio-catalogue/tur-asr002)

**Web-scraped large pools (YouTube-derived)**
- YODAS (ESPnet, YouTube CC-licensed videos): Turkish subsets `tr000` = 588.698 h (first shard digit 0 = "manual" captions) and `tr100` = 4,067.68 h (1 = automatic captions); licence cc-by-3.0; audio 16 kHz; the README warns that "manual caption only indicates that it is uploaded by users, but not necessarily transcribed by a human" — [espnet/yodas README](https://huggingface.co/datasets/espnet/yodas/raw/main/README.md)
- YODAS2 is the same collection re-released with audio "fixed to be 24k", total 60.3 TB, licence cc-by-3.0; the team "only downloaded videos with CC licenses"; no per-language table is visible on the card (durations are per-video fields) — [espnet/yodas2](https://huggingface.co/datasets/espnet/yodas2)
- A speech-restored YODAS2 derivative (24 kHz, tagged TTS) exists — [sarulab-speech/yodas2_sidon](https://huggingface.co/datasets/sarulab-speech/yodas2_sidon)
- YodaLingua-Turkish: 163 h, 56,422 audio–text pairs, 2,255 speakers, MP3 mono 24 kHz 16-bit; transcripts from "state-of-the-art ASR models"; noise reduction, diarization, VAD merge into 3–30 s; kept only if DNSMOS P.835 OVRL > 2.4; licence tag cc-by-4.0; gated (email request) — [Thomcles/YodaLingua-Turkish](https://huggingface.co/datasets/Thomcles/YodaLingua-Turkish)
- YODAS v3 (Sep 2026 preprint) reports over 1 million hours, high-bandwidth stereo, with Arabic and Welsh the top languages (~25k h each) and English under 2%; Turkish share not given in the excerpt — [YODAS v3, arXiv 2609.29448](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.29448)
- Emilia / Emilia-Large contain NO Turkish: languages are English, Chinese, German, French, Japanese, Korean (Emilia 101.7k h CC BY-NC 4.0; Emilia-YODAS 113.9k h CC BY 4.0; total 215.6k h) — [amphion/Emilia-Dataset](https://huggingface.co/datasets/amphion/Emilia-Dataset)

**Audiobook / long-form read speech on Hugging Face (scraped, machine transcripts)**
- serdarcaglar/turkish-tts-audiobooks: 2,724 h across 857,123 clips (train+validation = 1,311 h / 424,817 clips; `review` = 1,413 h / 432,306 unverified clips); 16 kHz mono FLAC in Parquet; audio from 24 public upload channels (2,370 source recordings, e.g. seslikitaplarmavi, BirDinle) "collected in mid-2026"; two channels ≈80% of training hours; 744 speaker *clusters* in train ("speaker_id is a cluster, not an identity"); transcripts from `openai/whisper-large-v3-turbo` in two passes (greedy + T=0.4), no human verification; a 180-clip human audit gave CER 0.0012 (clean) / 0.0080 (overall); digits and punctuation kept verbatim; licence `other` ("open-weights-with-attribution"); the card states source copyright "was not cleared"; gating requires citation and that any trained model's weights be released under a permissive licence (closed/API-only models prohibited) — [serdarcaglar/turkish-tts-audiobooks](https://huggingface.co/datasets/serdarcaglar/turkish-tts-audiobooks)
- serdarcaglar/kiraat: 1.84M clips, 3,105.7 h (2,575.2 h "recommended"), 90 speaker clusters, 27 channels, gated; read speech (listing data only; card not read) — [serdarcaglar/kiraat](https://huggingface.co/datasets/serdarcaglar/kiraat)
- risaleinur/risale-nur-audio: 91,792 clips, 203.02 h of religious-text readings (tr/ar/fa/ku) — [risaleinur/risale-nur-audio](https://huggingface.co/datasets/risaleinur/risale-nur-audio); sister set risaleinur/risalei-nur-text-audio is CC BY 4.0 — [listing](https://huggingface.co/api/datasets?filter=language:tr,task_categories:text-to-speech&limit=100)
- ysdede/khanacademy-turkish: 78 h, ≈27.1k rows (train 25.7k / test 1.36k), avg 10.5 s, MP3 mono 16 kHz, from the Khan Academy Turkish YouTube channel; transcripts processed with Turkish number-format localization + forced alignment/VAD; card body says CC BY-NC-SA 3.0 but the metadata tag says cc-by-sa-3.0 (conflict) — [ysdede/khanacademy-turkish](https://huggingface.co/datasets/ysdede/khanacademy-turkish)

**Podcast / conversational scrapes**
- Codyfederer/tr-podcast-dataset: 115,031 segments, 825 speakers, 37 GB WAV (original sample rate preserved); merged from 281 source sets via "Vyvo Dataset Builder"; automatic transcription + diarization (ASR model unnamed); music/noise filtering; auto-detected emotion labels happy / neutral / angry / sad; licence CC BY 4.0 (self-declared); gated — [Codyfederer/tr-podcast-dataset](https://huggingface.co/datasets/Codyfederer/tr-podcast-dataset)

**Single-speaker / studio / synthetic sets (TTS-tagged)**

- AIxBlock/Turkish-short-utterances: ≈299k sentences tagged TTS (content/licence not verified) — [listing](https://huggingface.co/api/datasets?filter=language:tr,task_categories:text-to-speech&limit=100)

**Pretrained open Turkish TTS models that imply data (for baselines)**
- facebook/mms-tts-tur: VITS, 36.3M params, licence CC-BY-NC 4.0; card does not state training source or hours (refers to arXiv 2305.13516) — [facebook/mms-tts-tur](https://huggingface.co/facebook/mms-tts-tur)
- XTTS was trained with 165.3 h of Turkish (of 27,281.6 h total); for non-English languages "most of the data are from the Common Voice dataset" — [XTTS paper, arXiv 2406.04904](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.04904v1)
- FreyaTTS (July 2026) is pretrained on "a large-scale, high-quality internal corpus of multi-speaker Turkish speech" (hours undisclosed) and voice-locked on "an internal single-speaker corpus of a single consented professional voice talent, recorded at 16 kHz over a narrowband channel"; the paper states that public corpora FLEURS-tr and Common-Voice-tr "offer only tens of hours of validated read speech" and that "no openly available corpus targets a conversational domain" — [FreyaTTS, arXiv 2607.09530](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09530)
- A Turkish NLP resource survey (2022) is the best single index of older academic Turkish speech resources — [Resources for Turkish NLP, arXiv 2204.05042](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2204.05042)

### Inferences
- For a *licence-clean* TTS pretraining set you are capped at roughly 130 h (CV25 validated) + ~12 h (FLEURS) + 10 h (MediaSpeech) + 5 h (antalia) ≈ 160 h of mostly read, crowd-mic speech; everything larger is YouTube/audiobook scraping with ASR transcripts and uncleared copyright, which is exactly the regime the user's TRT/Kanal D pipeline also sits in.
- The HF "TTS" tag for Turkish is dominated by (a) re-uploads of the same ~81k-sample synthetic merge and (b) 2026 synthetic "alania" sets distilled from other TTS systems; these are useful for codec/tokenizer pretraining or distillation but not as ground truth for expressive TTS.
- ISSAI TSC's MIT-vs-CC-BY conflict and undisclosed sources mean it should be treated as "research-grade, provenance unknown" rather than commercially clean.
- YODAS `tr000` (≈589 h with uploader captions) is the largest *CC-licensed* Turkish pool, but transcripts need re-ASR and alignment before TTS use.

### Gaps
- Common Voice 25.0 numbers could only be read via a search summary; the Data Collective page requires JavaScript (confirm hours/speakers directly before quoting).
- ISSAI TSC speaker count, sample rate and source (the MDPI paper returned HTTP 403) — unresolved.
- No per-language Turkish hours for FLEURS (`tr_tr` split sizes) were found; only the ≈12 h/language average.
- VoxLingua107 and CoVoST-2 Turkish hours were not retrieved (VoxLingua107 host refused the connection); both are ASR/LID-oriented anyway.
- serdarcaglar/kiraat and the "alania" sets were seen only through API listings, not their cards.
- No LibriVox-Turkish or Turkish Bible/Quran-reading corpus with an explicit TTS licence was found beyond risale-nur-audio; MMS-TTS-tur's training source is not documented on its card.

## Key Question 2: Which Turkish corpora carry emotion / style labels (angry, sad, happy, laughing, crying)?

### Takeaway
Turkish emotion-labelled speech is tiny and mostly acted or movie-derived: TurEV-DB (0.5 h, 4 emotions, 6 amateur actors), TurES (≈5.1k movie utterances, 7 categories + VAD, availability unclear), BAUM-1 (31 subjects, 8 emotions + 4 mental states, spontaneous Turkish). The only source with "laughing", "crying", "screaming", "whispering" labels in Turkish is Basis Global Emotions (6.12 h tr-TR, 72 speakers, licence restricts redistribution, human-reviewed gating); the podcast scrape has only *automatic* 4-class labels.

### Cited Findings
- TurEV-DB (METU, SLTU-CCURL 2020): "over 1735 tokens based on 82 words" in four emotions (angry, calm, happy, sad), produced by six amateur actors (three female, three male, early 20s to mid-30s), checked by assessors; per-emotion counts angry 487 / calm 408 / happy 357 / sad 483; recorded with the Sony Sound Recorder app in mono WAV at 44,100 Hz, denoised and trimmed in Audacity 2.3.0, exported as 32-bit float PCM; the paper (ELRA, CC-BY-NC) says "With the completion of the future work TurEV-DB will be made open to the public" — [TurEV-DB paper PDF](https://aclanthology.org/2020.sltu-1.52.pdf); METU record — [avesis.metu.edu.tr](https://avesis.metu.edu.tr/yayin/26f5bd4b-b309-422e-b4d0-bb791cf2e9fb/turkish-emotion-voice-database-turev-db)
- EmoBox (INTERSPEECH 2024) includes TurEV-DB as the only Turkish SER dataset among its 32: source "Act", 4 emotions, 6 speakers, 1,735 utterances, 0.5 h — [EmoBox, arXiv 2406.07162](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.07162v1)
- TurEV-DB Table 1 compares older Turkish emotion corpora: TurES 5,305 tokens / 582 actors / Turkish movies / afraid, angry, neutral, other, sad, surprised + valence-arousal-dominance; EmoSTAR 393 tokens / 393 actors / TV and Internet / angry, neutral, happy, sad; BUEMODB (Boğaziçi) 484 files / 11 non-professional actors / angry, happy, sad, unemotional; Bakır & Yuzkat "Voice Corpus" 3,740 samples / 50 speakers / afraid, angry, sad, happy, neutral — [TurEV-DB paper PDF](https://aclanthology.org/2020.sltu-1.52.pdf)
- TurES (Oflazoglu & Yildirim 2013): "5,100 utterances extracted from 55 Turkish movies", categorical labels (happy, surprised, sad, angry, fearful, neutral, other) plus valence/activation/dominance ratings; 4-class classification gave 45.5% unweighted average recall; valence correlation was low (search-summary; Springer page required a cookie redirect) — [EURASIP JASMP 2013](https://asmp-eurasipjournals.springeropen.com/articles/10.1186/1687-4722-2013-26); MKU record — [openaccess.mku.edu.tr](https://openaccess.mku.edu.tr/items/8c678a05-8ccc-48a4-8636-86ad252d3b2e/full). The utterance count differs across papers (5,100 vs 5,304/5,305).
- BAUM-1 (IEEE Trans. Affective Computing 8(3):300–313, 2017): spontaneous audio-visual Turkish; emotions happiness, anger, sadness, disgust, fear, surprise, boredom, contempt; mental states unsure (confusion/indecision), thinking, concentrating, bothered; subjects "speak about what they watched without a script" — [Marmara avesis record](https://avesis.marmara.edu.tr/yayin/4847b211-1f1f-46bc-b64c-32bca0ab6a7b/baum-1-a-spontaneous-audio-visual-face-database-of-affective-and-mental-states); the project page states clips were recorded from 31 subjects and also contains short acted recordings of each subject (search-summary; host refused connection) — [BAUM-1 project page](https://mimoza.marmara.edu.tr/~cigdem.erdem/BAUM1)
- BAUM-2 is described as a multilingual audio-visual affective face database with clips "from movies and TV programs" spanning a wide age range (search-summary; both repository pages returned 404/unreadable) — [Bahçeşehir record](https://acikerisim.bahcesehir.edu.tr/entities/publication/1f5c5117-2f4c-4cd0-9a74-6084c67ba689/full)
- Basis Global Emotions (2026): 525.17 h, 137,696 recordings, 10,420 speakers, 108 locales; Turkish tr-TR = 6.12 h, 1,601 recordings, 643 series, 72 speakers; 38 "steers" including affectionate, afraid, amused, angry, apologetic, bored, calm, confused, **crying**, disappointed, disgusted, dramatic, embarrassed, excited, frustrated, happy, **laughing**, mumbling, sad, sarcastic, **screaming**, **shouting**, singing, soothing, surprised, **whispering**; each series also has a neutral reading; scripted lines performed in described situations (instructed performances); 48 kHz 16-bit mono WAV, no post-processing; "Basis Data License 1.0" permits research and commercial use/training but forbids redistributing recordings or identifying speakers; access requests reviewed by a person — [basis-ai/basis-global-emotions](https://huggingface.co/datasets/basis-ai/basis-global-emotions)
- Codyfederer/tr-podcast-dataset carries automatically detected emotion labels (happy, neutral, angry, sad) on 115,031 segments — [tr-podcast-dataset](https://huggingface.co/datasets/Codyfederer/tr-podcast-dataset)
- Emilia / Emilia-Large (the main "in-the-wild expressive" pretraining corpus) has no Turkish — [amphion/Emilia-Dataset](https://huggingface.co/datasets/amphion/Emilia-Dataset)
- dots.tts (Aug 2026) built style/emotion control from a ≈7k h "caption-paired set" with Gemini-generated descriptions of speaker traits, emotion, delivery and environment, on top of 1.5M h; its listed open-source corpora (Emilia, LibriTTS-R, HiFi-TTS, WenetSpeech4TTS, AISHELL-3, Magicdata, MLS, MSR-86K, IndicVoices-R, EuroSpeech, WaxalNLP-TTS, FLEURS) include no Turkish-specific set — [dots.tts Technical Report, arXiv 2606.07080](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.07080)

### Inferences
- No open Turkish corpus teaches non-verbal vocalizations (laughter, crying) at scale; Basis Global Emotions is the only labelled source and it is 6 h / 72 speakers under a no-redistribution licence, so it is a fine-tuning or evaluation set, not a pretraining set.
- TurES and BAUM-2 are themselves movie/TV-derived, so they share the copyright posture of the user's broadcast pipeline; TurEV-DB is single-word tokens (not sentence TTS data).
- The practical route used by 2026 systems (dots.tts, OmniVoice) is to *caption* in-the-wild audio with emotion/style descriptions automatically rather than rely on human-labelled corpora; for Turkish this would have to be done on the scraped pools above (podcast, audiobook, TRT).

### Gaps
- Could not confirm BAUM-2 clip/subject counts or whether BAUM-1/2 are still distributed (licence agreement pages unreachable).
- Could not confirm whether TurES is downloadable today (no URL/licence found).
- EmoNet-Voice / LAION "Got Talent" language coverage was not checked in this pass (no Turkish mention surfaced in any result).

## Key Question 3: Legal / licence considerations for TV-broadcast-derived training data in Turkey and for releasing weights (factual summary, not legal advice)

### Takeaway
Turkish copyright law (Law No. 5846, FSEK) has no text-and-data-mining or research-scraping exception and a closed exception list, gives broadcasters and performers neighbouring rights (Art. 80), and a draft amendment introducing extended collective licensing and an AI-training licensing mechanism was pending but not confirmed enacted as of these searches; separately, the KVKK Board's 2026 principle decision treats biometric data including "ses tınısı" (voice timbre) as special-category personal data requiring an Art. 6 legal basis.

### Cited Findings
- "No TDM or research scraping exception exists in Turkish copyright law — Law 5846's exception list is closed and excludes automated mass extraction"; Annex Article 8 creates a sui generis database-producer right (15 years) that Turkish courts have recognised (search-summary attribution) — [law.ungovr.org/ai/tr](https://law.ungovr.org/ai/tr)
- Commentary that AI systems "based on data scraping for the creation of their algorithms could be considered in violation of the Copyright Law" (interpretive, not a ruling) — [Global Legal Post, Turkey AI chapter](https://www.globallegalpost.com/lawoverborders/artificial-intelligence-1549732605/turkey-1214342480)
- A draft amendment to Law 5846 before the TBMM "introduces extended collective licensing systems for digital uses and establishes a novel two-way licensing mechanism specifically addressing AI systems' use of copyrighted content during both training and output generation phases"; enactment not confirmed — [Gün + Partners, Key Developments and Predictions for Copyright Law in Turkey](https://gun.av.tr/insights/updates/key-developments-and-predictions-for-copyright-law-in-turkey); general IP overview — [Chambers IP 2026, Turkey](https://gpg-pdf.chambers.com/Intellectual-Property-2026/362/)
- A Turkish academic article on using works for AI training compares the EU TDM exception, Japan's non-enjoyment exception and US/Singapore fair use, and concludes the issue is unresolved under existing Turkish legislation — [İZÜ open access](https://openaccess.izu.edu.tr/entities/publication/f8aa042b-f82b-43bf-bc76-3f7c1d8f23cd/full)
- Turkish-language practitioner commentary: no explicit statute or case law yet on training on copyrighted works without permission; "FSEK m.38 fair use Türk hukukunda dar kapsamlıdır" and unlicensed training raises an FSEK m.71 infringement debate — [hukukcularevi.com](https://hukukcularevi.com/danismanlik/yapay-zeka-ve-telif-hakki/); [ffkpartnerhukuk 2026 guide](https://www.ffkpartnerhukuk.com.tr/yapay-zeka-ile-uretilen-iceriklerin-telif-hakki-2026-guncel-rehber-ai-ve-fikri-mulkiyet-hukuku/)
- Neighbouring (bağlantılı) rights under FSEK cover performers, phonogram producers, radio-television organisations and film producers; broadcasters must contract with rights holders / collecting societies and pay fees to use works, performances and phonograms (per the regulation issued under Arts. 43, 25 and 80) — [TBB Dergisi article on neighbouring rights](https://tbbdergisi.barobirlik.org.tr/ViewPDF-eser-sahibinin-haklari-ile-baglantili-haklar-155); official English text of Law 5846 — [WIPO Lex](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/en/legislation/details/11397)
- Personal data: the KVKK Board's Principle Decision No. 2026/921 (29.04.2026, Official Gazette 02.06.2026, clarified by a 27.08.2026 announcement) restates that biometric data are special-category data under Art. 6 requiring one of the listed legal bases and extra security measures, lists fingerprint, face, iris/retina and "ses tınısı" (voice timbre) among biometric data, and holds that explicit consent alone may be insufficient where less intrusive alternatives exist (context: workplace attendance) — [CottGroup summary](https://www.cottgroup.com/en/component/zoo/item/kvkk-ilke-karari-mesai-takibi-amaciyla-biyometrik-veri-islenmesi); [Milliyet report](https://www.milliyet.com.tr/teknoloji/kvkkdan-isverenlere-uyari-parmak-izi-ve-yuz-tanimayla-mesai-takibi-bitiyor-mu-7598095)
- Pending Turkish bills on AI content: a 7 Nov 2025 bill amending the Penal Code would require labelling AI-generated content and removal of personality-rights-violating content within 6 hours — [Tamindir](https://www.tamindir.com/haber/yapay-zeka-kanun-teklifi_101579/); an MHP proposal would amend KVKK (Law 6698) to penalise unauthorised sharing of AI-generated voice, image and text — [Bengütürk](https://www.benguturk.com/gundem/mhpden-yapay-zeka-hamlesi-teklif-ismail-ozdemirden-geldi-sahte-iceriklere-ceza-255430h)
- Practice on Hugging Face: the largest Turkish audiobook scrape states copyright "was not cleared", grants no rights over audio or text, and conditions access on releasing trained weights under a permissive licence and not cloning voices without consent — [serdarcaglar/turkish-tts-audiobooks](https://huggingface.co/datasets/serdarcaglar/turkish-tts-audiobooks); the Khan Academy scrape carries CC BY-NC-SA 3.0 (non-commercial) — [ysdede/khanacademy-turkish](https://huggingface.co/datasets/ysdede/khanacademy-turkish); FreyaTTS avoided the issue by training on internal corpora and a "consented professional voice talent" — [FreyaTTS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09530)

### Inferences
- Broadcast audio (TRT, Kanal D) implicates at least three rights layers in Turkey: the underlying work (script/programme), the performers' neighbouring rights (voice actors, presenters), and the broadcaster's own neighbouring right; absent a TDM exception, none of these is cleared by "publicly accessible" status.
- Releasing weights trained on such audio is a separate act from private research use; the community datasets above try to manage this with gating and open-weights clauses, but these are self-imposed terms, not licences from the rights holders.
- Voice data of identifiable presenters/actors is personal data under KVKK; speaker embeddings used for voice cloning arguably fall under the biometric ("ses tınısı") category per the 2026 Board decision.

### Gaps
- Could not confirm the current status (enacted / pending / withdrawn) of the FSEK amendment with the AI licensing mechanism; TBMM search surfaced only unrelated AI bills.
- No TRT-specific archive terms or any Turkish court decision on AI training data were found.
- Attribution of the "closed exception list / Annex Art. 8" quotes to specific pages comes from a search summary; verify against the pages before citing in a formal document.

## Key Question 4: How Turkish / multilingual TTS is evaluated, which Turkish ASR to score with, and reported Turkish numbers for existing systems

### Takeaway
The de-facto 2025–2026 protocol is: WER/CER from Whisper-large-v3 (band-matched at 8 kHz in the Turkish-specific Freya protocol), speaker similarity from WavLM-SV / WavLM-based ECAPA-TDNN (SIM-o) or ECAPA-TDNN cosine, UTMOS as a naturalness proxy (acknowledged as English-trained), plus CMOS/SMOS or MOS listening tests with native raters; Turkish rows exist in MiniMax-Speech, OmniVoice, XTTS and Freya-TR-Eval, with Turkish WER ranging 0.7% (ElevenLabs on MiniMax's set) to 83% (SpeechT5-tr on Freya), and Whisper-large-v3's own Turkish floor is ≈2.4–5.2% CER / ≈8–14% WER depending on test set.

### Cited Findings

**Protocols used by multilingual TTS papers**
- XTTS (INTERSPEECH 2024): CER via Whisper Large v3, SECS via the ECAPA2 speaker encoder, UTMOS as objective naturalness; 240 random FLORES+ devtest sentences per language; 20 DAPS speakers (10M/10F) with one 3–8 s English reference each, used cross-lingually; subjective CMOS/SMOS on −2..+2 with ≥15 evaluators and ≥8 samples each — [XTTS paper HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.04904v1)
- MiniMax-Speech 24-language test set: 100 sentences per language, two Common Voice speakers per language, Whisper-large-v3 for all languages except Chinese — [MiniMax-Speech, arXiv 2505.07916](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.07916)
- OmniVoice (Apr 2026, 600+ languages, 581k h open-source data): SIM-o from a WavLM-based ECAPA-TDNN; WER/CER with Hubert-based ASR for LibriSpeech-PC, Paraformer-zh for Chinese, the Omnilingual ASR model for the FLEURS-Multilingual-102 benchmark, and Whisper-large-v3 for the rest; UTMOS; CMOS (−3..3) and SMOS (0..5); defines "FLEURS-Multilingual-102" (dev/test of FLEURS) as the widest zero-shot TTS benchmark; notes OmniVoice "has exceeded the measurement capability of existing ASR models" (its CER is below ground-truth CER in many languages) — [OmniVoice, arXiv 2604.00688](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.00688)
- dots.tts (Aug 2026): WER for non-Chinese languages, CER for Chinese, SIM = cosine of WavLM-SV embeddings between generated speech and prompt; "de facto ASR convention": Whisper-Large-v3 for English and multilingual benchmarks, Paraformer for Mandarin, "per-benchmark default ASR for the remaining languages on the MiniMax multilingual set"; benchmarks Seed-TTS-Eval, MiniMax-Multilingual-24, CV3-Eval, EmergentTTS-Eval — [dots.tts, arXiv 2606.07080](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.07080)
- Voxtral TTS (Mar 2026): WER via Voxtral Mini Transcribe v2, UTMOS-v2, ECAPA-TDNN cosine similarity; 9-language table, Turkish not visible — [Voxtral TTS, arXiv 2603.25551](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.25551)
- Accent Vector (Mar 2026) uses Whisper-medium transcripts and wavlm-base-plus-sv embeddings — [arXiv 2603.07534](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.07534)
- Chatterbox (Resemble) reports no numeric per-language scores; its Turbo model was evaluated with Podonos preference studies (overall preference, naturalness, expressiveness) vs ElevenLabs Turbo v2.5, Cartesia Sonic 3, VibeVoice 7B; Chatterbox Multilingual supports 23 languages including Turkish (tr); all outputs carry the PerTh neural watermark — [Chatterbox README](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/resemble-ai/chatterbox@master/README.md); v3 claims 25 languages incl. 4 dialects and 6 "Language Pack" models — [Resemble blog](https://www.resemble.ai/resources/chatterbox-multilingual-v3-tts-with-embedded-watermarking-for-25-languages)

**Turkish-specific benchmark: Freya-TR-Eval (July 2026)**
- 495 natural, domain-neutral conversational Turkish sentences (statements, *mI*-questions, exclamations; 3–13 words, mean ≈8), merged from Common Voice 17-tr + CoVoST2-tr text ("short-native") and Gemini-3.1-pro-generated lines under topic/type quotas ("everyday"); filtered for full Turkish letter coverage (ç ğ ı i ö ş ü); CC-BY-4.0; metrics: primary "semantic WER" (ASR re-transcription + LLM judge counting only meaning-changing errors), secondary band-matched WER/CER (all systems downsampled to 8 kHz before Whisper), naturalness via UTMOS or human MOS; card warns that standard Whisper WER "mixes intelligibility with Whisper's own Turkish error rate", penalises narrowband systems, and is affected by digit-normalization artifacts, and that UTMOS "is an English-trained proxy that penalizes narrowband audio"; a leaderboard Space `freyavoice/Freya-TR-Eval-Leaderboard` exists — [freyavoice/freya-tr-eval](https://huggingface.co/datasets/freyavoice/freya-tr-eval)
- FreyaTTS paper protocol: WER/CER from Whisper-large-v3 after 8 kHz band-matching with identical Turkish text normalization on reference and hypothesis; MOS from 24 native Turkish raters, 2,566 ratings (360–375 per system), 5-point, 95% CIs; voice consistency = F0 std across regenerations; content similarity = 1 − normalized char edit distance vs Whisper transcript; speaker lock verified with ECAPA-TDNN cosine (0.873 ± 0.029 vs 0.883 ± 0.026 same-speaker ceiling, 0.140 different-speaker floor) — [FreyaTTS, arXiv 2607.09530](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09530)
- FreyaTTS Table 2 (sub-1B open Turkish systems, band-matched Whisper WER / CER / MOS): Piper tr-dfki (16M) 4.4 / 1.1 / 3.47; Coqui GlowTTS-tr (28M) 12.1 / 3.3 / 2.53; MMS-TTS-tr (36M) 6.8 / 1.7 / 3.58; SpeechT5-tr (144M) 83.4 / 45.5 / 1.59; FreyaTTS (183.2M) 8.0 / 3.0 / 3.68; F5-TTS-Turkish (336M) 24.3 / 10.9 / 3.63; XTTS-v2 (470M) 11.1 / 3.9 / 3.82; the 95% MOS intervals of the top four overlap; calibration anchors: *real* FLEURS-tr test recordings transcribe at 9.7% WER under the same pipeline, and re-encoding real speech through the frozen AudioVAE alone costs 2.6% WER — [FreyaTTS, arXiv 2607.09530](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09530)
- EMA-TTS (65M, Apache-2.0, single speaker) self-reports on Freya-TR-Eval WER 3.0 / CER 0.8 (3.03 ± 0.24 / 0.81 ± 0.06 over three seeds) with competitor numbers copied from the FreyaTTS table; ASR judge and MOS not stated — [canberkkkkkk/ema-tts](https://huggingface.co/canberkkkkkk/ema-tts)
- Derived evaluation artefacts on HF: PatientdeskAI/freya-tr-eval-rerun (3 takes per system), erayack/freya-tts-mlx-eval (990 WAVs), Ertugrul1611/akil-tts-tr-eval (4,020 per-clip results on Chatterbox systems), yagmurtuncer/turkasr-bench-tts (7,884 clips, 13.1 h, 9 TTS engines) — [HF TTS listing](https://huggingface.co/api/datasets?filter=language:tr,task_categories:text-to-speech&limit=100)

**Reported Turkish numbers for commercial / large open systems**
- MiniMax-Speech Table 2, Turkish: MiniMax-Speech WER 1.520 / SIM 0.779 vs ElevenLabs Multilingual v2 WER 0.699 / SIM 0.596 — [MiniMax-Speech](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.07916)
- OmniVoice Table 3 (MiniMax-Multilingual-24), Turkish: OmniVoice WER 2.166 / SIM-o 0.851; MiniMax 1.520 / 0.779; ElevenLabs 0.699 / 0.596; 24-language averages OmniVoice 2.850 / 0.830, MiniMax 3.774 / 0.766, ElevenLabs 10.950 / 0.655 — [OmniVoice](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.00688)
- XTTS Table 4, Turkish: XTTS CER 1.042 / SECS 0.5031 vs YourTTS 2.6367 / 0.4855 (16-language averages 2.0646 / 0.5046 vs 4.7949 / 0.4704) — [XTTS paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.04904v1)
- CosyVoice 3 abstract lists "9 languages and 18 Chinese dialects" (≈1M training hours) without naming them — [CosyVoice 3, arXiv 2505.17589](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.17589); third-party listings name the 9 as English, Chinese, Japanese, Korean, German, Spanish, French, Italian, Russian (no Turkish) — [ai-bot.cn CosyVoice page](https://ai-bot.cn/cosyvoice/)
- No published Turkish WER/SIM for Chatterbox, Google Cloud TTS or Azure Neural TTS was found (a community Chatterbox benchmark covered only en/de/ar/es) — [HF ResembleAI/chatterbox discussion](https://huggingface.co/ResembleAI/chatterbox/discussions/61)

**Turkish ASR models for scoring**
- Whisper-large-v3 zero-shot Turkish CER: 2.42% (FLEURS), 5.15% (Common Voice 25), 4.21% combined (normalized); a fine-tuned large-v3 ("BuzzASR/turkish", MIT, trained on FLEURS + CV25) reaches CER 2.0 / WER 7.99 on FLEURS and CER 4.67 / WER 13.91 on CV25; the card claims lowest combined CER among Whisper-large-v3, Omnilingual 1B/7B, MMS, Qwen3-ASR and Cohere Transcribe but gives no per-model numbers; normalization procedure not described — [BuzzASR/turkish](https://huggingface.co/BuzzASR/turkish)
- Whisper-large-v3-turbo fine-tune "RsGoksel_ITU_Mainframe": 9.77% WER on the Common Voice 17 Turkish test split (9,650 utterances) vs 13.09% for zero-shot large-v3-turbo under the same pipeline; author-measured; CC-BY-NC-4.0; references a community `ysdede/turkish_asr_leaderboard` — [RsGoksel/RsGoksel_ITU_Mainframe](https://huggingface.co/RsGoksel/RsGoksel_ITU_Mainframe)
- ysdede/whisper-khanacademy-large-v3-turbo-tr: WER 15.70 on the Khan Academy Turkish test split (self-reported, MIT) — [model card](https://huggingface.co/ysdede/whisper-khanacademy-large-v3-turbo-tr)
- Whisper large-v2 FLEURS Turkish WER ≈8.4% (third-party table citing the Whisper paper's appendix; v2 not v3) — [VexaScribe summary](https://vexascribe.com/how-accurate-is-whisper)
- FreyaTTS re-transcribes with Whisper-large-v3 and reports that real FLEURS-tr speech scores 9.7% WER under its band-matched pipeline (i.e., the recognizer's own floor is comparable to the best TTS systems' WER) — [FreyaTTS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09530)
- OmniVoice uses Meta's Omnilingual ASR for its FLEURS-102 benchmark and reports its average CER (4.00%) *below* ground truth (5.11%) — [OmniVoice](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.00688)

### Inferences
- A credible "beats existing Turkish TTS" claim in 2026 needs at least: (a) Freya-TR-Eval band-matched Whisper-large-v3 WER/CER with the published scripts, (b) a zero-shot cloning row on the MiniMax-24 Turkish subset (Common Voice prompts, Whisper-large-v3, WavLM-SV/SIM-o) so numbers are comparable with MiniMax/ElevenLabs/OmniVoice, (c) a native-rater MOS or CMOS with CIs, and (d) an explicit statement of the text normalizer used, because Turkish WER is dominated by digit/abbreviation normalization and by Whisper's own ≈8–14% Turkish WER floor.
- Because Whisper-large-v3's Turkish error floor is high relative to the best systems, CER (not WER) and/or a Turkish-fine-tuned Whisper (BuzzASR, ITU_Mainframe) plus an LLM-judged semantic WER are the more discriminative intelligibility measures; any claim should report both the raw ASR and the recognizer's floor on real speech.
- UTMOS/DNSMOS/NISQA values are not calibrated for Turkish; FreyaTTS and Freya-TR-Eval both fall back to human MOS for naturalness, and the open sub-1B field (Piper, MMS, XTTS-v2, FreyaTTS) is within overlapping MOS CIs, so pairwise CMOS is required to separate systems.
- No public Turkish leaderboard with commercial systems (ElevenLabs v3, Google, Azure) exists; the only cross-vendor Turkish numbers are from vendor-run papers (MiniMax, OmniVoice) on MiniMax's private 100-sentence set.

## Key Question 5: Turkish-specific text-processing pitfalls and available normalization / G2P tools

### Takeaway
Turkish TTS front-ends break on number/date/currency expansion, abbreviations, acronyms, the dotted/dotless I casing, vowel-harmonised suffixes on digits and foreign words, and the *mI* question particle; the existing open tools are partial (num2words `tr`, espeak-ng `tr`, epitran `tur-Latn`, XTTS's small Turkish cleaner, Zemberek morphology, a 2016 rule-based G2P), NeMo WFST normalization has no documented Turkish support, and the 2026 Turkish-first models sidestep G2P by training on characters while still expanding digits in the frontend.

### Cited Findings
- FreyaTTS states that "agglutinative morphology, vowel harmony, and the spoken-form expansion of numbers, dates, and acronyms make hand-built grapheme-to-phoneme and normalization pipelines brittle and incomplete"; it uses a 92-symbol character vocabulary (29-letter alphabet incl. ç ğ ı ö ş ü + digits/punct/space) with no phonemizer/G2P, but "digit strings are expanded to their spoken form at the text frontend" because the duration predictor cannot handle orthographic-vs-phonetic length mismatch; isolated acronyms and bare tokens were a documented failure mode fixed by a "short-utterance-coverage" fine-tune on forced-aligned one/two-word segments (torchaudio MMS_FA with a Turkish-to-roman character map) and inference guards (clause chunking, duration floor, voicing retry) — [FreyaTTS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09530)
- Freya-TR-Eval deliberately includes *mI*-questions and screens for "isolated numbers, brands, and bank or customer terms"; it notes WER "is affected by digit-normalization artifacts" — [freya-tr-eval](https://huggingface.co/datasets/freyavoice/freya-tr-eval)
- XTTS tokenizer (Coqui TTS) Turkish path: abbreviation expansions only for `b.`→"bay", `byk.`→"büyük", `dr.`→"doktor"; symbol map `&`→"ve", `%`→"yüzde", `$`→"dolar", `£`→"sterlin", `°`→"derece", `#`→"diyez", `@`→"at"; ordinal regex `([0-9]+)(\.|inci|nci|uncu|üncü|\.)` (test "Bu 1. test"→"Bu birinci test"); numbers via `num2words(lang="tr")` with thousands-dot removal; decimal expansion skipped for Turkish (`if lang != "tr"`) and two Turkish decimal/thousands tests are commented out as not working; currency handled by generic USD/GBP/EUR regexes in a bare try/except; pre-lowercasing maps İ→i, Ö→ö, Ü→ü but does NOT map I→ı; character limit 226 for `tr` (warning only) — [TTS/tts/layers/xtts/tokenizer.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/coqui-ai/TTS/dev/TTS/tts/layers/xtts/tokenizer.py)
- num2words supports `tr`; conversion types cardinal, ordinal, ordinal_num, year, currency; LGPL — [num2words on PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/num2words/)
- epitran lists `tur-Latn` (plus `tur-Latn-bab` and `tur-Latn-red`), MIT licence, "mapping-and-repairs" approach; its language-space table notes Turkish is "Based on data with suffixes attached"; no accuracy figure for Turkish — [epitran GitHub](https://github.com/dmort27/epitran)
- espeak-ng supports Turkish (`tr`, family `trk`) with no footnoted caveats — [espeak-ng languages.md](https://github.com/espeak-ng/espeak-ng/blob/master/docs/languages.md); Piper's Turkish voices (used as the strongest small baseline in FreyaTTS) are "phonemizer-driven VITS" — [FreyaTTS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09530)
- Zemberek-NLP (Java, Apache-2.0): morphology (analysis, disambiguation, generation), tokenization + sentence boundary, basic spell check and noisy-text normalization; no number-to-words, abbreviation or G2P module; "slow maintenance mode", last release 0.17.1 (23 July 2019) — [zemberek-nlp GitHub](https://github.com/ahmetaa/zemberek-nlp)
- A rule-based Turkish G2P (Altınok, 2016) outputs SAMPA and derives stress from morphological analysis — [arXiv 1601.03783](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1601.03783v1)
- NVIDIA NeMo-text-processing's README does not mention Turkish and the language-support page returned 404; no evidence of Turkish TN/ITN grammars — [NeMo-text-processing GitHub](https://github.com/NVIDIA/NeMo-text-processing)
- The ACL 2019 paper on normalizing non-canonical Turkish describes the state of the art as "a token-level pipeline of modules that relies heavily on external linguistic resources and manually defined rules" (social-media normalization, not TTS) — [Çolakoğlu et al., ACL 2019](https://aclanthology.org/P19-2037)
- Field report: a Vapi user found Turkish TTS reading "3+1" in English because an English-centric number normalizer overrode the Turkish locale; the vendor's workaround was to disable voice formatting — [Vapi community thread](https://vapi.ai/community/m/1447889126128091186)
- The Khan Academy Turkish corpus explicitly applied "number-format localization for Turkish" to its transcripts — [ysdede/khanacademy-turkish](https://huggingface.co/datasets/ysdede/khanacademy-turkish)
- OmniVoice avoids language-specific normalization entirely by using the pre-trained LLM's subword tokenizer, "eliminating cumbersome grapheme-to-phoneme conversion and language-specific text normalization", and adds phoneme override only for Chinese (pinyin) and English (CMUdict) — [OmniVoice](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.00688)

### Inferences
- There is no maintained, TTS-grade Turkish text normalizer; a developer must assemble one from num2words (cardinals/ordinals/years/currency) plus custom rules for dates, times, units, abbreviations, acronym spelling, and vowel-harmony-correct suffixes on numerals ("3'te"→"üçte", "1990'da"→"bin dokuz yüz doksanda"), and must handle İ/I casing explicitly because generic lowercasing corrupts "ı".
- For evaluation, the *same* normalizer must be applied to reference and ASR hypothesis (as FreyaTTS does), otherwise digit expansion alone can swing Turkish WER by several points.
- Character-level models (FreyaTTS, EMA-TTS, OmniVoice) show that G2P can be dropped for Turkish's largely phonemic orthography, but ğ (vowel lengthening), loanword pronunciation, and acronyms still need either data coverage or an override mechanism.

### Gaps
- No quantitative accuracy comparison of espeak-ng vs epitran vs Altınok G2P on Turkish was found.
- No open Turkish date/time/unit normalizer (beyond number words) was found; NeMo Turkish support could not be confirmed either way.
- Stress placement and ğ handling in espeak-ng's Turkish rules were not examined.
