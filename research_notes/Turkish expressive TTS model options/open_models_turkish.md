# Open-source / open-weight TTS models with Turkish support — landscape as of October 2026

Scope note: "Official Turkish" below means Turkish (`tr`) appears in the model's own card/README language list. "Community" means a third-party fine-tune. All release dates, parameter counts and licenses are taken from the cited primary pages; where a page did not state a value it is marked "not stated". Models from 2023–2024 are explicitly marked OLDER.

## Key Question 1 — Which notable open TTS models list Turkish (official vs community), and what are their specs?

### Takeaway
As of October 2026 only a handful of general-purpose open-weight models list Turkish officially: XTTS-v2 (2023, non-commercial), Bark (2023, MIT, research-grade), MMS-TTS-tur (2023, VITS, CC-BY-NC), Piper (3 `tr_TR` voices, MIT code), Chatterbox Multilingual V3 (MIT), VoxCPM2 (Apache-2.0, 30 languages), Fish Audio S2 Pro (`tr` in "other supported", non-commercial), Higgs TTS 3 / Higgs Audio v3 4B (Turkish in its Tier-1 list, non-commercial), and OmniVoice (646 languages, CC-BY-NC weights). The most-cited current-generation open models — Qwen3-TTS, CosyVoice 3, Ming-3.0, Breeze TTS 2, Voxtral TTS, Kokoro, Orpheus, IndexTTS-2, Zonos, Llasa, Spark-TTS, MeloTTS, Dia/Dia2, CSM, Kyutai, Step-Audio-EditX, Magpie — do NOT list Turkish. A new wave of Turkish-only open models appeared in 2026, almost all built on or distilled from VoxCPM2 (Trendyol-TTS, Ahenk, EMA Lightning, Nefes) plus the independent FreyaTTS.

Kur0Keri/turkce-tts-vits Turkish-only VITS, Apache-2.0 — [HF Kur0Keri/turkce-tts-vits](https://huggingface.co/Kur0Keri/turkce-tts-vits). gokbilge-tts VITS/Piper on ISSAI corpus — [GitHub gokbilge/gokbilge-tts](https://github.com/gokbilge/gokbilge-tts).

### Inferences
- The "official Turkish" set among modern (2025–2026) general models reduces to four practically relevant bases: VoxCPM2 (only one that is both Apache-2.0 and ships LoRA/SFT scripts), Chatterbox Multilingual V3 (MIT but no fine-tuning code and weakest in the Sestek objective eval), Higgs TTS 3 and Fish S2 Pro (both strong multilingual claims but non-commercial weights).
- The entire 2026 Turkish open-model ecosystem converged on VoxCPM2: Trendyol-TTS and Ahenk are VoxCPM2 LoRAs, EMA Lightning was trained on 1,000 h of VoxCPM2-synthesized speech, and Nefes wraps VoxCPM2 — a strong signal that VoxCPM2 is the de-facto Turkish-capable base in the community.
- Parameter counts in HF metadata are often inflated versus marketing names (Qwen3-TTS 1.7B→2B, Orpheus 3B→4B, Higgs 4B→5B, Llasa 3B→4B, Ming 0.5B→1B); the paper/marketing numbers are used above.
- OmniVoice almost certainly covers Turkish (646 languages; Sestek fine-tuned and ran it zero-shot on Turkish), but the page did not expose the list.

### Gaps
- Drama 3's 83-language list was not exposed on the card, so Turkish support is unconfirmed; its license name is internally inconsistent on the card.
- Sample rates for Chatterbox, S2 Pro, Qwen3-TTS, Trendyol-TTS, MMS-TTS-tur, Piper and several others are not stated on the fetched pages (values I "know" from training were deliberately not written down).
- No source gave Bark's total parameter count, and no Turkish-specific quality evidence for Bark was found anywhere.
- Maya1 (Elo 1047), Supertonic-3, KittenTTS-2, gradium phonon-40m-multilingual and AuroraTTS appear on trending/leaderboards but were not researched for Turkish.
- bounjee/turkish-voice-kit advertises a 10-model, 189-sentence Turkish benchmark with results in `results/`, but neither the model list nor numbers were visible on the README; the linked dolphydesk.com write-up could not be located — [GitHub bounjee/turkish-voice-kit](https://github.com/bounjee/turkish-voice-kit).

## Key Question 3 — What happened to Coqui (XTTS license) and Suno Bark?

### Takeaway

## Key Question 4 — Which models have good vs poor Turkish pronunciation/prosody, and what evidence exists?

### Takeaway
Three Turkish-specific evaluations exist in 2026: Sestek's 18-metric objective study (Chatterbox, CosyVoice-3-FT, OmniVoice, VoxCPM2, VITS; no global winner, Chatterbox weakest and unstable on short inputs, VoxCPM2 best speaker fidelity, fine-tuned CosyVoice best learned naturalness), the FreyaTTS 7-system human-MOS study (XTTS-v2 highest MOS 3.82 but 11.1% WER; Piper lowest WER 4.4%; F5-TTS-Turkish 24.3% WER; SpeechT5 unusable), and Ahenk's TR-Zor hard-sentence WER benchmark (Trendyol-TTS best naturalness, 8.6M CPU models reach ~2.3% WER). Documented Turkish-specific failure modes are digits/abbreviations/suffix agreement (fixed by normalizers like trnorm/tr-speech), question intonation without "mı", and letter "c" misreads under English-language instructions; vowel harmony and ı/i are reported as learnable from raw characters.

### Cited Findings
- Sestek (Ankara) objective study, arXiv 2610.06057, 5 Oct 2026, code github.com/EmreOzkose/tr-tts-eval: systems Chatterbox multilingual (zero-shot), CosyVoice (cited as CosyVoice 3, fine-tuned on 2,000 h EN/AR/TR/UR, 22 speakers; raw-text and phoneme front-ends), OmniVoice (fine-tuned and zero-shot), VoxCPM2 (zero-shot), VITS (20 h single speaker); 300 Turkish utterances (100 short/medium/long) from a 48 kHz internal set; 18 metrics, no WER, no human MOS. Headline numbers: UTMOSv2 — CosyVoice 3.441, OmniVoice-FT 3.129, OmniVoice-ZS 3.105, Chatterbox 3.092, VITS 3.064, Gold 3.079, VoxCPM2 2.886; speaker similarity — Gold 0.894, VoxCPM2 0.881, CosyVoice 0.878, VITS 0.875, Chatterbox 0.873, OmniVoice-ZS 0.870, OmniVoice-FT 0.843; DNSMOS-Pro — VITS 4.225 (above gold 3.601); SQUIM SI-SDR — OmniVoice-ZS 28.5. Chatterbox wins no metric and collapses on short input (SI-SDR 26.4→20.4, speaker sim 0.889→0.825, WhisQA 4.58→3.61). Long-form identity drift: VoxCPM2 first-to-last Δ −0.0003 (near zero), CosyVoice −0.0094, Chatterbox −0.0613. Phoneme vs raw-text front-end for CosyVoice: differences small and mixed; authors conclude phonemization gives little benefit because Turkish orthography is largely transparent — [arXiv 2610.06057](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.06057v1).
- FreyaTTS Freya-TR-Eval (495 conversational sentences; Whisper-large-v3 with 8 kHz band-matching; MOS from 24 native raters): Piper tr-dfki 16M WER 4.4 / CER 1.1 / MOS 3.47; Coqui GlowTTS-tr 28M 12.1 / 3.3 / 2.53; MMS-TTS-tr 36M 6.8 / 1.7 / 3.58; SpeechT5-tr 144M 83.4 / 45.5 / 1.59; FreyaTTS 183M 8.0 / 3.0 / 3.68; F5-TTS-Turkish 336M 24.3 / 10.9 / 3.63; XTTS-v2 470M 11.1 / 3.9 / 3.82. Efficiency on RTX 4090: FreyaTTS RTF 0.11, F5 0.13, XTTS 0.33, Spark-TTS 1.08, VoxCPM2 0.37 (0.15 with serving engine, 11.4 GB). Observations: FreyaTTS learns vowel harmony and dotted/dotless ı from characters without G2P; digit strings are a failure mode (expanded at the frontend); acronyms were spelled letter-by-letter until SFT; short/bare-token utterances failed in early checkpoints — [arXiv 2607.09530 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.09530).
- Ahenk TR-Zor (155 hard sentences, 13 categories; judge faster-whisper large-v3-turbo; UTMOS22): Ahenk WER 2.54 (141/155 perfect) UTMOS 3.70; Trendyol-TTS 3.98 (134/155) UTMOS 3.82 (best naturalness; held-out WER 0.68 best); Ahenk Cep 2.32 (144/155) 3.44; EMA Lightning+trnorm 2.43 3.36; Antalia-2 Mini 3.30 3.23. trnorm reduced EMA Lightning's TR-Zor error from 14.0% to 1.9% (different judge setup). Pronunciation notes: questions with "mı" correct, questions without "mı" ("Yarın geliyorsun?") end like statements in both VoxCPM2-based models; with English instructions a leading "c" may be read as /k/, /s/, /z/ ("Cam"→"Kam"), Turkish instructions fix it (blind test 10/10); some Ahenk emotional clips "bright and crackly" (7 of 20); no MOS study — [GitHub ozguradmin/ahenk-turkish-tts](https://github.com/ozguradmin/ahenk-turkish-tts).
- EMA Lightning card reports Freya-TR-Eval WER 0.92% (3 seeds, 0.79–1.05%), CER 0.18%, UTMOS ~3.30 "good, not the best"; changelog 1.1.x improved brand names and abbreviations — [HF canberkkkkkk/ema-lightning](https://huggingface.co/canberkkkkkk/ema-lightning).
- Turkish number/suffix normalization is a recurring requirement: bounjee `tr-speech` converts prices, dates, times, phone numbers, IBANs, e-mails and abbreviations and applies the correct suffix to the spoken number ("14:00'e" → "on dörde") — [GitHub bounjee/turkish-voice-kit](https://github.com/bounjee/turkish-voice-kit); trnorm makes suffixes "follow the spoken form" after number/abbreviation conversion — [GitHub ahenk](https://github.com/ozguradmin/ahenk-turkish-tts); Trendyol-TTS warns of drops on abbreviations, unusual names, numbers and long text — [HF Trendyol/Trendyol-TTS](https://huggingface.co/Trendyol/Trendyol-TTS).
- XTTS-v2 Turkish: "fluid" output with correct vowel harmony "in most cases", rated 5/5 naturalness; Glow-TTS "intelligible but noticeably more robotic"; article notes vowel harmony is hard for phoneme-based systems and Turkish prosody differs from Indo-European languages — [ablt.dev, 19 May 2026](https://www.ablt.dev/blog/turkish-tts/).
- SpeechT5 Turkish fine-tune card: WER 0.1538 after fine-tuning vs 1.0077 base, with Turkish-specific letters ğ and ş the weakest point — [HF thealper2/speecht5-tts-turkish](https://huggingface.co/thealper2/speecht5-tts-turkish).
- Chatterbox V3 Turkish CER in the ~1–5% band; Resemble cautions a model "can score well on transcription-based metrics while still missing prosody" — [Resemble AI blog](https://www.resemble.ai/resources/chatterbox-multilingual-v3-tts-with-embedded-watermarking-for-25-languages). An informal blog test reports Turkish "quality consistent with other languages" (single listener) — [sonusahani.com](https://sonusahani.com/blogs/chatterbox-multilingual); Wiro's sample used plain-ASCII Turkish so it cannot validate ı handling — [Wiro](https://wiro.ai/blog/chatterbox-multilingual-5-language-tts-samples/).
- Higgs TTS 3 self-reports Turkish in its <5 WER/CER tier, with no per-language table published — [HF higgs-audio-v3-tts-4b](https://huggingface.co/bosonai/higgs-audio-v3-tts-4b).
- Nefes 0.5 (VoxCPM2 pipeline) on 10 unseen Turkish sentences: WER 11% mean, 6/10 at 0% — [HF alicangunduz/nefes-0.5](https://huggingface.co/alicangunduz/nefes-0.5).
- VoxCPM2's own docs warn "performance may vary across languages depending on training data availability" — [voxcpm.readthedocs.io](https://voxcpm.readthedocs.io/en/latest/_sources/index.rst.txt).

### Inferences
- Ranking of Turkish quality evidence by strength: (1) human MOS in FreyaTTS (XTTS-v2 > FreyaTTS ≈ F5-TTS-TR ≈ MMS > Piper > GlowTTS >> SpeechT5) — but XTTS and F5 have high WER, i.e. natural but error-prone; (2) Sestek objective study (fine-tuned CosyVoice 3 and VoxCPM2 strongest, Chatterbox weakest); (3) Ahenk/EMA automatic WER (VoxCPM2-LoRA models and tiny distilled models achieve 1–4% WER on hard sentences).
- "Good" Turkish today: VoxCPM2 and its LoRAs (Trendyol-TTS, Ahenk), fine-tuned CosyVoice 3, FreyaTTS, EMA Lightning (intelligibility), XTTS-v2 (naturalness only). "Poor"/unproven: Chatterbox Multilingual on Turkish (no metric wins, short-utterance collapse), F5-TTS community fine-tune (24% WER), SpeechT5 fine-tunes, Glow-TTS, Bark (no evidence).
- Because Turkish orthography is transparent, character-level/tokenizer-free models (VoxCPM2, FreyaTTS) learn ı/i and vowel harmony without G2P; the remaining risk is text normalization (numbers, abbreviations, suffix agreement) and sentence-level prosody (questions without the "mı" particle), which no current model is documented to solve.

### Gaps
- No source evaluates lexical stress or ğ lengthening explicitly for any modern model; "ğ" is only mentioned for SpeechT5 fine-tunes.
- No Turkish human-listening comparison includes any 2026 LLM-codec model (Higgs, S2 Pro, Drama 3, Ming-3.0, Qwen3-TTS); Higgs's Turkish tier is self-reported.
- bounjee's 10-model Turkish benchmark results were not readable.
- No r/LocalLLaMA thread specifically on Turkish TTS surfaced in searches.

### Inferences
- For a fine-tuned Turkish model intended for commercial use, the only base that is simultaneously (a) officially Turkish, (b) permissively licensed and (c) shipped with fine-tuning code is VoxCPM2. - Chatterbox is MIT but Resemble publishes no fine-tuning recipe and embeds a watermark; it is deployable as-is but not an obvious fine-tuning base.
- Non-commercial leaders (Higgs TTS 3, S2 Pro, Drama 3) can serve as quality references or synthetic-data teachers only if their licenses permit that use — not verified here.

### Gaps
- Whether Higgs/Fish/Breeze licenses permit using outputs as training data for other models was not checked.
- The NVIDIA Open Model License, Fish Audio Community License and Bilibili Model License texts were not read.
- No source states whether Trendyol's dataset owner permits commercial redistribution of the merged weights.

## Key Question 6 — Ranked top candidates for Turkish quality (synthesis)

### Takeaway
For expressive, commercially usable Turkish TTS in October 2026 the evidence points to VoxCPM2 (and its LoRAs Trendyol-TTS/Ahenk) first, with fine-tuned Fun-CosyVoice3 as the strongest alternative base; Higgs TTS 3 and Fish S2 Pro are likely the best raw-quality Turkish-capable open weights but are non-commercial and unmeasured on Turkish by third parties; Chatterbox V3 is the easiest MIT drop-in but scored weakest in the only independent Turkish study; XTTS-v2 remains the human-preferred legacy baseline; EMA Lightning/Ahenk Cep/FreyaTTS/Piper are the CPU tier.

### Cited Findings
- VoxCPM2: official Turkish, Apache-2.0, LoRA from 5–10 min — [HF](https://huggingface.co/openbmb/VoxCPM2); best speaker similarity (0.881) and near-zero identity drift in Sestek eval, but lowest UTMOSv2 (2.886) zero-shot — [arXiv 2610.06057](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.06057v1); Turkish LoRAs reach TR-Zor WER 2.5–4.0 and UTMOS up to 3.82 — [GitHub ahenk](https://github.com/ozguradmin/ahenk-turkish-tts); Ahenk adds 6 emotions + laughter — same source.
- Fun-CosyVoice3 fine-tuned: best UTMOSv2 (3.441, above gold 3.079), SCOREQ, WhisQA, TTSDS on Turkish — [arXiv 2610.06057](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.06057v1); Apache-2.0, training + GRPO code — [GitHub CosyVoice](https://github.com/FunAudioLLM/CosyVoice).
- Higgs TTS 3: Turkish Tier 1 (self-reported), top open-weights in 4/9 arena languages, non-commercial — [HF](https://huggingface.co/bosonai/higgs-audio-v3-tts-4b), [Artificial Analysis on X](https://x.com/ArtificialAnlys/status/2102490340678856997).
- Fish S2 Pro: `tr` supported, 15k emotion tags, fine-tuning code, Elo 1109, non-commercial — [HF](https://huggingface.co/fishaudio/s2-pro), [Artificial Analysis](https://artificialanalysis.ai/text-to-speech/leaderboard/provider-voice/open-weights).
- Chatterbox V3: Turkish CER 1–5%, MIT — [Resemble](https://www.resemble.ai/resources/chatterbox-multilingual-v3-tts-with-embedded-watermarking-for-25-languages); no metric wins, short-input collapse — [arXiv 2610.06057](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.06057v1).
- XTTS-v2: highest human MOS 3.82 of 7 Turkish systems, WER 11.1 — [arXiv 2607.09530](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.09530); non-commercial — [discussion #4145](https://github.com/coqui-ai/TTS/discussions/4145).
- CPU tier: EMA Lightning WER 0.92% RTF 0.0023 — [HF](https://huggingface.co/canberkkkkkk/ema-lightning); Ahenk Cep WER 2.32 RTF 0.2 — [GitHub ahenk](https://github.com/ozguradmin/ahenk-turkish-tts); FreyaTTS MOS 3.68 RTF 0.70 on M3 CPU — [HF](https://huggingface.co/freyavoice/freya-tts); Piper dfki WER 4.4 MOS 3.47 — [arXiv 2607.09530](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.09530).

### Inferences
- Proposed ranking for "Turkish expressive quality × usability": (1) VoxCPM2 + custom LoRA (best combination of license, Turkish support, emotion/voice-design control and proven community recipes; weakness: zero-shot naturalness and RTF >1 at 16 steps on A100 without serving engine); (2) Fun-CosyVoice3-0.5B fine-tuned on tens of hours of Turkish (best measured naturalness, Apache-2.0, streaming); (3) Higgs TTS 3 (likely highest raw quality, research-only); (4) Fish S2 Pro (expressive tags, research-only); (5) Chatterbox Multilingual V3 (MIT, zero effort, mediocre Turkish); (6) OmniVoice fine-tuned (fast, 646 languages, CC-BY-NC); (7) XTTS-v2 (legacy, non-commercial); (8) EMA Lightning / Ahenk Cep / FreyaTTS-small / Piper for CPU; (9) MMS-TTS, Glow-TTS, F5-TTS-Turkish, SpeechT5, Bark — not recommended.
- For a project that owns a Turkish broadcast corpus, the actionable path with the most evidence is LoRA/SFT on VoxCPM2 (Trendyol used ~20 h; Ahenk used a synthetic set) or full fine-tuning of Fun-CosyVoice3 (Sestek used 2,000 h multilingual), with a Turkish text normalizer (trnorm or tr-speech) in front.

### Gaps
- No head-to-head human evaluation includes VoxCPM2-LoRA, CosyVoice3-FT, Higgs TTS 3, S2 Pro and Chatterbox together on Turkish; the ranking above combines three non-comparable studies.
- Minimum data requirements for a good Turkish LoRA on VoxCPM2 are undocumented beyond the card's "5–10 minutes" (voice adaptation) and Trendyol's "~20 hours".
