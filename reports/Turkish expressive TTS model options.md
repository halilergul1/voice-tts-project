# Build Turkish expressive TTS on VoxCPM2

As of October 2026 only one open-weight base is simultaneously fluent in Turkish, permissively licensed, shipped with fine-tuning scripts, and able to design new voices and steer emotion from text: OpenBMB's **2B-parameter VoxCPM2 (Apache-2.0, 30 languages including Turkish, LoRA and SFT scripts)** ([HF openbmb/VoxCPM2](https://huggingface.co/openbmb/VoxCPM2)). The entire 2026 Turkish open-model wave (Trendyol-TTS, Ahenk, EMA Lightning, Antalia-2 Mini, Nefes) is either a VoxCPM2 LoRA or was trained on VoxCPM2-generated speech, which makes it the de-facto Turkish base by community consensus as well as by elimination. The independent evidence on Turkish quality is thin but consistent: Sestek's 18-metric study found VoxCPM2 best at speaker fidelity (**0.881 vs 0.894 for real speech**) yet weakest on UTMOSv2 zero-shot, a fine-tuned CosyVoice 3 the most natural, and Chatterbox the weakest and unstable on short inputs ([arXiv 2610.06057](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.06057v1)). Intelligibility is already saturated: on the only single-harness Turkish leaderboard an 8.6M-parameter CPU model scores **1.04% WER** and Trendyol-TTS **1.16%**, both ahead of ElevenLabs v4 at 1.43% ([PatientDesk re-run](https://huggingface.co/datasets/PatientdeskAI/freya-tr-eval-rerun)), so "beating every Turkish TTS" is winnable only on human-rated naturalness, emotion control and non-verbal sounds, dimensions that no Turkish system has measured with listeners. The recommended path is a staged **rank-64 LoRA on VoxCPM2 (roughly 20 GB VRAM at the documented batch, inside the RTX 5090's 32 GB)** ([VoxCPM fine-tuning guide](https://voxcpm.readthedocs.io/en/latest/finetuning/finetune.html)) trained on the music-separated, re-normalised Engelsiz corpus, with emotion and gender labels manufactured by an emotion2vec+/EmpathicInsight ensemble calibrated on a few hundred native-rated clips and laughter/sigh/cry tags mined by sound-event detectors; Fun-CosyVoice3-0.5B full fine-tuning is the fallback if naturalness plateaus once the corpus passes roughly 200 hours. The two largest risks are not computational: no open model has demonstrated crying or human-validated emotion control in Turkish, and Turkish copyright law gives broadcasters and performers neighbouring rights with no text-and-data-mining exception, which argues for designed rather than cloned voices and a legal review before any weights leave the lab.

## Only four modern open models speak Turkish, and the 2026 ecosystem converged on VoxCPM2

The leaderboard winners are not Turkish speakers. On the Artificial Analysis open-weights arena the top tier is Fish Drama 3 Preview (Elo 1246), Breeze TTS 2 (1230), Ming-3.0-tts (1189), Fish S2 Pro (1109), Step-Audio-EditX (1100) and Voxtral TTS (1090) ([Artificial Analysis](https://artificialanalysis.ai/text-to-speech/leaderboard/provider-voice/open-weights)), yet Qwen3-TTS, CosyVoice 3, Ming-3.0, Breeze TTS 2, Voxtral, Kokoro, Orpheus, IndexTTS-2, Zonos, Llasa and Dia all omit Turkish from their language lists ([HF Qwen3-TTS](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-TTS-12Hz-1.7B-Base); [GitHub CosyVoice](https://github.com/FunAudioLLM/CosyVoice); [HF Ming-3.0](https://huggingface.co/inclusionAI/Ming-3.0-tts-0.5B)). Among current-generation general models, Turkish appears officially only in VoxCPM2, Chatterbox Multilingual V3 (MIT, 23 languages, Turkish in the vendor's "1–5% CER" band) ([Resemble blog](https://www.resemble.ai/resources/chatterbox-multilingual-v3-tts-with-embedded-watermarking-for-25-languages)), Higgs TTS 3 (4B, Turkish self-reported in its sub-5 WER/CER Tier 1, research-only licence) ([HF higgs-audio-v3-tts-4b](https://huggingface.co/bosonai/higgs-audio-v3-tts-4b)), Fish S2 Pro (5B, `tr` among "other supported languages", non-commercial research licence) ([HF fishaudio/s2-pro](https://huggingface.co/fishaudio/s2-pro)), and OmniVoice (0.6B, 646 languages, weights CC-BY-NC) ([HF k2-fsa/OmniVoice](https://huggingface.co/k2-fsa/OmniVoice)). The 2023 generation (XTTS-v2, Bark, MMS-TTS-tur, Piper) still lists Turkish, but XTTS-v2's weights are non-commercial under the Coqui Public Model License ([coqui-ai/TTS discussion #4145](https://github.com/coqui-ai/TTS/discussions/4145)) and Bark has had no dated update since May 2023 and no Turkish quality evidence anywhere ([GitHub suno-ai/bark](https://github.com/suno-ai/bark)).

Three Turkish-specific evaluations exist, none of which includes a 2026 LLM-codec leader, and they disagree in instructive ways. Sestek's SPECOM 2026 study ran Chatterbox and VoxCPM2 zero-shot, CosyVoice and OmniVoice fine-tuned on about 2,000 hours across English, Arabic, Turkish and Urdu, and a 20-hour VITS baseline, over 300 Turkish utterances and 18 metrics with no WER and no human MOS; it found "no single winner": fine-tuned CosyVoice led UTMOSv2 (**3.441, above the 3.079 of real recordings**), SCOREQ, WhisQA and TTSDS, VoxCPM2 led speaker similarity (0.881) and NatScore with a first-to-last identity drift of −0.0003 over long passages versus −0.0613 for Chatterbox, and Chatterbox won no metric while collapsing on short inputs (speaker similarity 0.889 to 0.825, WhisQA 4.58 to 3.61) ([arXiv 2610.06057](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.06057v1)). VoxCPM2's zero-shot UTMOSv2 of 2.886 was the lowest in that table, which is the single most important caveat on the primary recommendation: the base is a better *speaker* than it is a *performer* until it is fine-tuned. The FreyaTTS paper's 24-rater, 2,566-rating MOS study of seven sub-1B or legacy systems put XTTS-v2 first at **MOS 3.82 but 11.1% WER**, FreyaTTS-small at 3.68/8.0%, F5-TTS-Turkish at 3.63 with a disqualifying 24.3% WER, Piper tr-dfki at 3.47 with the lowest WER of 4.4%, and SpeechT5 fine-tunes at 1.59/83.4%; the top four MOS intervals overlapped ([arXiv 2607.09530](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.09530)). The third source is the community WER race described in the next section, where VoxCPM2 LoRAs and tiny VoxCPM2-distilled models dominate.

Two further points settle the choice. VoxCPM2 is the only Turkish-capable open system with *documented* text-only voice design, scoring 84.2/83.2/71.4 (APS/DSD/RP) on InstructTTSEval in English with I-MOS 4.50 and N-MOS 4.48 from 50 listeners ([arXiv 2606.06928](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.06928v1)); the other model with that capability, Qwen3-TTS-VoiceDesign, does not speak Turkish. And VoxCPM2's controllable cloning puts emotion, rate, pitch and energy in a plain text prefix "requiring no additional embedding modules" ([arXiv 2606.06928](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.06928v1)), which means the Engelsiz transcripts can carry emotion conditioning as ordinary text without touching the architecture. Its weaknesses are equally concrete: an RTF of 0.37 on an RTX 4090 (0.15 with the serving engine, 11.4 GB) ([arXiv 2607.09530](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.09530)), the low zero-shot UTMOSv2 above, and the docs' own warning that "performance may vary across languages depending on training data availability" ([VoxCPM docs](https://voxcpm.readthedocs.io/en/latest/_sources/index.rst.txt)).

## Turkish builders saturated intelligibility with synthetic speech but never measured expressiveness

Between March 2025 and October 2026 Turkish developers published well over 40 Turkish TTS checkpoints on Hugging Face, and the two waves differ sharply. The 2025 wave was Orpheus (Llama-3B + SNAC) and F5-TTS fine-tunes on Common Voice 17 or scraped YouTube narrators, mostly undocumented; its best-documented result, a Red Hat engineer's Orpheus LoRA on 20,000 samples (r=32, lr 2e-5, 8 epochs, two A100 80 GB), reached a self-described "proof of concept" Whisper-small WER of 0.72 ([HF AbDhumal](https://huggingface.co/AbDhumal/orpheus-3b-turkish-tts-v2)), while the Common Voice F5 fine-tune scored 24.3% WER under FreyaTTS's protocol and its author told a user that four hours is too little and "dozens of hours" are needed ([HF marduk-ra discussion](https://huggingface.co/marduk-ra/F5-TTS-Turkish/discussions/2)). The 2026 wave pivoted to VoxCPM2 in two ways. As a LoRA target: Trendyol (the e-commerce group) merged a rank-64 LoRA trained for 2,000 steps on a private set of roughly 20 hours and ships it with `cfg_value=2.0` and 16 inference steps ([HF Trendyol-TTS](https://huggingface.co/Trendyol/Trendyol-TTS); [PyPI turkish-tts](https://pypi.org/project/turkish-tts/)); mramazan trained rank-64/alpha-64 on about 18 hours of a single speaker to step 1,400 with trnorm normalisation, −23 LUFS loudness and 3–20 s clips, and reports that "stability issues may occur with very short (<3 seconds) or highly expressive input texts" ([HF mramazan](https://huggingface.co/mramazan/voxcpm2-turkish-tts-lora)); and Ahenk added a 36.2M-parameter LoRA with **six emotions plus laughter on a single voice designed from a text description**, released at step 500 ([HF ozgurguler/ahenk-tts-tr](https://huggingface.co/ozgurguler/ahenk-tts-tr)). As a teacher: PatientDesk AI released Alania, **3,411 hours of VoxCPM2-synthesised speech in 2,752 designed voices** ([HF alania-synthetic-speech-tr](https://huggingface.co/datasets/cloud0day3/alania-synthetic-speech-tr)), and within three weeks of each other three 8M-parameter single-voice models trained on roughly 1,000–1,600 hours of such synthetic audio appeared (EMA Lightning, Antalia-2 Mini, Ahenk Cep), with EMA Lightning alone collecting 63,008 downloads ([HF ema-lightning](https://huggingface.co/canberkkkkkk/ema-lightning); [HF antalia-mini](https://huggingface.co/cloud0day3/antalia-mini)).

The consequence is a WER race that has hit the recogniser's floor. PatientDesk's single-harness re-run of 20 systems on Freya-TR-Eval (495 conversational sentences, Whisper-large-v3 after 8 kHz band-matching, three takes on one L4) ranks EMA Lightning 1.04%, Antalia-2 Mini 1.09%, the Alania-2 API 1.15%, Trendyol-TTS 1.16% with the highest UTMOS of all systems at 3.82, Gemini 3.8 Flash 1.23%, Anka 1.42%, ElevenLabs v4 1.43%, Chatterbox Multilingual 1.91%, Piper 3.41%, XTTS-v2 3.67%, FreyaTTS-small 12.02% and the F5 base 77.84% ([PatientDesk re-run](https://huggingface.co/datasets/PatientdeskAI/freya-tr-eval-rerun)). Under FreyaTTS's own pipeline *real* FLEURS-tr recordings transcribe at 9.7% WER and merely re-encoding real speech through the frozen AudioVAE costs 2.6% ([arXiv 2607.09530](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09530)), so sub-2% differences between systems are inside the ASR's noise. Ahenk's TR-Zor benchmark of 155 hard sentences (numbers, dates, prices, phone numbers) separates systems more usefully: Ahenk 2.54% WER, Ahenk Cep 2.32%, EMA Lightning with trnorm 2.43%, Antalia-2 Mini 3.30% and Trendyol-TTS 3.98%, with Trendyol still best on naturalness (UTMOS 3.82) and on 20 held-out long sentences (0.68%) ([GitHub ahenk-turkish-tts](https://github.com/ozguradmin/ahenk-turkish-tts)). What nobody has done is a listening test on a 2026 model: the only human evaluations in the whole Turkish field are Freya's 24-rater study of legacy systems and Antalia-1's single-session identity check, and every author who reports UTMOS flags it as an English-trained proxy ([HF freya-tr-eval](https://huggingface.co/datasets/freyavoice/freya-tr-eval)).

The recipes that worked share a set of lessons that transfer directly to the Engelsiz corpus. Text normalisation is the first: every card from Orkhon to Antalia-1 to erkamk says digits must be verbalised, Antalia-1 reports that "numbers, normalization-heavy text, foreign names and abbreviations have the highest error rates" ([HF antalia-1](https://huggingface.co/cloud0day3/antalia-1)), trnorm cut EMA Lightning's TR-Zor error from 14.0% to 1.9%, and bounjee's tr-speech applies the vowel-harmonised suffix to the spoken number ("14:00'e" to "on dörde") ([GitHub turkish-voice-kit](https://github.com/bounjee/turkish-voice-kit)). Tokenizer coverage is the second: IndexTTS-2.5's Turkish LoRA went from **12.36% to 1.32% WER solely by training 1,530 never-updated text-embedding rows that carried 37% of Turkish token occurrences** (v1 rendered "yağmur" as "arkadaş mur") ([HF berrkkaayy](https://huggingface.co/berrkkaayy/IndexTTS-2.5-Turkish-LoRA)), and SpeechT5's English SentencePiece lacked every one of ç ğ ı İ ö ş ü ([HF thealper2](https://huggingface.co/thealper2/speecht5-tts-turkish)). Accent is the third and the strongest argument against a Chinese/English-centric base: on Qwen3-TTS an attention-only rank-64 LoRA "hits a ceiling on accent reduction" ([hcfk FINDINGS](https://huggingface.co/hcfk/qwen3-tts-turkish/raw/main/FINDINGS.md)) and even a full one-epoch adaptation on 2,277 hours of Kiraat audiobooks left the model "under-trained rather than converged" with garbled digits and unstable code-switching ([HF erkamk](https://huggingface.co/erkamk/qwen3-0.6b-tr)). Prosody is the fourth: both VoxCPM2 LoRAs render questions without the "mı" particle "like statements", a leading "c" is misread as /k/ or /s/ under English-language instructions but not under Turkish ones (10/10 in a blind test) ([GitHub ahenk-turkish-tts](https://github.com/ozguradmin/ahenk-turkish-tts)), and drifting-tts had to add a stochastic pitch model because its intonation was "the same flat average" ([HF drifting-tts-tr](https://huggingface.co/Vyvo/drifting-tts-tr)). Overfitting is the fifth: an in-domain DACVAE fine-tune improved its training monitor but regressed on Freya-TR-Eval and was not published ([HF dacvae-tts-tr-w512](https://huggingface.co/VoiceHub/dacvae-tts-tr-w512)), which is why every successful VoxCPM2 LoRA was stopped early (steps 500 to 2,000).

The data landscape explains the pivot to synthesis and also exposes the opening. Licence-clean real Turkish speech is small: Common Voice 25.0 holds about 129 validated hours from 1,816 speakers ([Mozilla Data Collective](https://datacollective.mozillafoundation.org/datasets/cmn2e7kbl01k2mm07gm5n1bc9)), FLEURS adds roughly 12 hours per language ([arXiv 2205.12446](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.12446)), and the only consented studio voice is Antalia's 5.008 hours ([HF antalia-voice-corpus](https://huggingface.co/datasets/cloud0day3/antalia-voice-corpus)). The pools above 1,000 hours are scraped audiobooks whose card states the source copyright "was not cleared" and whose gating demands permissive weight release ([HF turkish-tts-audiobooks](https://huggingface.co/datasets/serdarcaglar/turkish-tts-audiobooks)), YODAS `tr000` at about 589 hours of uploader-captioned CC-licensed YouTube audio ([espnet/yodas README](https://huggingface.co/datasets/espnet/yodas/raw/main/README.md)), and the synthetic Alania set, whose 24 kHz subset has "very little speech-rate variety (chars/sec IQR roughly 15–18)" ([HF alania-tr-250h-24k](https://huggingface.co/datasets/alperiox/alania-tr-250h-24k)). The Orpheus authors warn that synthetic voices "lack diversity" and produce worse fine-tunes ([GitHub Orpheus-TTS](https://github.com/canopyai/Orpheus-TTS)). That is precisely the gap a real, emotionally varied, multi-speaker drama corpus fills: the Turkish community has intelligibility and one-voice naturalness covered, and nobody has a large body of real emotional Turkish speech with aligned transcripts.

## Emotion and non-verbal control is cheap to add, but Turkish labels must be manufactured

Open models control expressiveness through four mechanisms, and the choice of base fixes which one the project inherits. Inline tags learned as transcript tokens drive Orpheus (`<laugh>`, `<sigh>`, `<gasp>` and five more), Fish S1-mini and S2 Pro, Bark, Dia and CosyVoice 3's `[laughter]`/`[breath]` ([GitHub Orpheus-TTS](https://github.com/canopyai/Orpheus-TTS); [HF s2-pro](https://huggingface.co/fishaudio/s2-pro); [arXiv 2505.17589](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.17589v2)); natural-language instructions prepended to the text drive CosyVoice 3, Qwen3-TTS and VoxCPM2 ([arXiv 2601.15621](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.15621); [arXiv 2606.06928](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.06928v1)); disentangled emotion embeddings with a gradient-reversal speaker classifier drive IndexTTS2, trained on a 135-hour emotional subset of 361 speakers after 55K hours of pretraining ([arXiv 2506.21619](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.21619)); and inference-time knobs drive Chatterbox and Zonos. Because VoxCPM2's mechanism is text, teaching it Turkish emotion labels is a data-formatting problem: Ahenk already did this for six emotions and laughter, Nefes shows Turkish tags such as `[gül]`, `[iç çek]` and `[fısılda]` can be used with VoxCPM2 in a training-free pipeline (though it splices non-verbals from a Maya1-generated bank rather than having VoxCPM2 produce them) ([HF nefes-0.5](https://huggingface.co/alicangunduz/nefes-0.5)), and Ahenk's own card notes that 7 of 20 emotional clips were "bright or crackly" and that breath sounds remain in pauses ([HF ozgurguler/ahenk-tts-tr](https://huggingface.co/ozgurguler/ahenk-tts-tr)). That is the full extent of the evidence for emotional Turkish on an open model: self-reported, single-voice, no listening test.

The data budget for adding expressiveness is better documented, albeit in English and Chinese. Tag-level control needs surprisingly little: Unsloth's Elise set is about three hours of one English speaker with 336 "laughs" and 156 "sighs" in the transcripts, and a few epochs on it changed Orpheus's voice and taught the tags together ([Unsloth TTS guide](https://unsloth.ai/docs/basics/text-to-speech-tts-fine-tuning)). Category-level emotion needs tens to a couple of hundred hours: NonverbalTTS's 17 hours of VoxCeleb and Expresso with 10 non-verbal types and 8 emotions brought fine-tuned open models to parity with CosyVoice2 on non-verbal fidelity ([arXiv 2507.13155](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.13155)), IndexTTS2 used 135 hours, and EmoVoice used roughly 240 hours of synthetic emotional speech ([arXiv 2504.12867](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.12867v3)). Free-form instruction following from scratch is a different regime altogether, 5,000 hours of 1 million for CosyVoice 3 and "tens of thousands of hours" of expressive speech for VoxCPM2 ([arXiv 2505.17589](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.17589v2); [arXiv 2606.06928](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.06928v1)), but the project does not need to build that: it needs to *specialise* a model that already has it. Crying is the hard case. Among open systems only Fish S1-mini lists sobbing and "crying loudly" as markers ([HF openaudio-s1-mini](https://huggingface.co/fishaudio/openaudio-s1-mini)), Step-Audio-EditX lists crying as future work ([GitHub Step-Audio-EditX](https://github.com/stepfun-ai/Step-Audio-EditX)), and no fetched source reports a cross-lingual transfer experiment for any non-verbal tag; a Turkish cry token will have to be taught from mined broadcast segments with no precedent to lean on.

Labels do not exist for the Engelsiz corpus and no labelling tool reports Turkish accuracy, so the labels have to be manufactured by an ensemble and calibrated by humans, which is exactly how the production labs do it. VoxCPM2 screened expressive utterances with lightweight emotion classifiers, had Step-Audio R1 and Gemini 2.5 Pro write descriptions, then ran expert classifiers for gender, age and emotion ([arXiv 2606.06928](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.06928v1)); NonverbalTTS chained a BEATs audio tagger, ASR, an emotion classifier, a fusion step and human validation ([arXiv 2507.13155](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.13155)). The open building blocks are emotion2vec+ large (9 classes, fine-tuned on 42,526 hours of pseudo-labelled speech, utterance- or 50 Hz frame-level via FunASR at 16 kHz) ([HF emotion2vec_plus_large](https://huggingface.co/emotion2vec/emotion2vec_plus_large)), whose predecessor was validated on 18 datasets in 10 languages with 84.34% on German EmoDB and 90.91% on Bangla SUBESCO ([ACL Findings 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-acl.931.pdf)); LAION's EmpathicInsight-Voice (40 emotion intensities plus valence/arousal) at **Spearman 0.415–0.418 against expert raters, on par with Gemini 2.5 Pro's 0.417** and 70.6%/74.2% zero-shot on EmoDB/RAVDESS ([arXiv 2506.09827](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.09827)); jrgillick's Switchboard-trained laughter detector that outputs time-stamped segments ([GitHub laughter-detection](https://github.com/jrgillick/laughter-detection)); and Qwen3-Omni-30B-A3B-Captioner for free-form style descriptions, with the caveat that its delivery captions "can be biased and overly positive" ([HF Qwen3-Omni Captioner](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-Omni-30B-A3B-Captioner); [arXiv 2601.10513](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.10513)). SenseVoice emits `<|Laughter|>`, `<|Cry|>` and `<|Breath|>` events but its released checkpoint covers only zh/en/yue/ja/ko ([GitHub SenseVoice](https://github.com/FunAudioLLM/SenseVoice)), so it is a secondary vote at best. Expect only the coarse classes to be trustworthy: EmoVoice dropped disgust, fear and surprise from its metric "because of low recognition accuracy" of emotion2vec, CosyVoice 3 saw sad/angry accuracy fall sharply when the text was neutral ([arXiv 2504.12867](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.12867v3); [arXiv 2505.17589](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.17589v2)), and six psychology-trained raters reached a Cronbach's alpha of only 0.14 on 40-class intensities ([arXiv 2506.09827](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.09827)). The user's three target emotions (angry, sad, neutral) are, fortunately, the three most reliably detected. Gender is easier still: a Data-Speech-style pipeline that bins pitch relative to same-gender speakers, paired with a gender classifier reported at F1 99.93%, is enough to add a gender attribute per speaker cluster ([GitHub dataspeech](https://github.com/huggingface/dataspeech); [arXiv 2509.14627](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.14627)).

Turkish emotional corpora exist only as calibration material. TurEV-DB is 1,735 single-word tokens of 82 words in four emotions from six amateur actors, about half an hour, and the only Turkish set in EmoBox ([TurEV-DB](https://aclanthology.org/2020.sltu-1.52.pdf); [arXiv 2406.07162](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.07162v1)); TurES has about 5,100 utterances cut from 55 Turkish movies with seven categories plus valence/activation/dominance from 27 annotators but no findable download or licence ([TurES](http://www.turesdatabase.com/page/dataset)); and Basis Global Emotions is the only source with Turkish "laughing", "crying", "screaming" and "whispering" labels, **6.12 hours from 72 speakers**, under a licence that permits training but forbids redistribution and is gated by human review ([HF basis-global-emotions](https://huggingface.co/datasets/basis-ai/basis-global-emotions)). Together they are a few hundred to a few thousand clips, which is the right size for measuring how well the automatic labellers agree with native Turkish ears before any label is trusted for training.

## One RTX 5090 fits a LoRA on a 2B model; preprocessing and law are the binding constraints

The VRAM arithmetic is favourable for the primary path and unfavourable for the alternatives. VoxCPM's guide gives rough figures at batch 16 and 8,192 max batch tokens of **about 20 GB for VoxCPM2 LoRA and about 40 GB for full fine-tuning**, prescribes LoRA learning rate 1e-4 with rank 32 for cloning and 64 "for style/language", full-fine-tune rate 1e-5, 1–3 epochs for a single speaker, and example configs of 1,000 steps ([VoxCPM fine-tuning guide](https://voxcpm.readthedocs.io/en/latest/finetuning/finetune.html)); its FAQ adds that overfitting where the model ignores the input text can appear "within a few hundred steps on small datasets", that `training_cfg_rate=0.1` should be kept, and that a *new* language wants "full fine-tuning with 500+ hours" with zh/en mixed in, which does not apply because Turkish is already in the base ([VoxCPM FAQ](https://voxcpm.readthedocs.io/en/latest/finetuning/faq.html)). A controlled study on Khmer and Korean found one shared zero-initialised LoRA on the language model and decoder with rank 64 best on 25.5 hours (Khmer MOS 3.85 to 4.23, p<0.001) at 0.19–3.03% trainable parameters ([arXiv 2606.26618](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26618)). Consumer-GPU precedents at corpus scale exist: IndexTTS-2.5's 486-hour Turkish LoRA ran on an RTX 5070 Ti plus an RTX 4070 ([HF berrkkaayy](https://huggingface.co/berrkkaayy/IndexTTS-2.5-Turkish-LoRA)), drifting-tts trained 150k steps in 13.6 hours on one RTX 5090 ([HF drifting-tts-tr](https://huggingface.co/Vyvo/drifting-tts-tr)), and a Fish S2 Pro LoRA ran on Blackwell silicon with torch 2.8.0+cu128 ([fish-speech issue #1346](https://github.com/fishaudio/fish-speech/issues/1346)). No source gives an RTX 5090 timing for VoxCPM2 or CosyVoice training, so every wall-clock figure in the plan below is an extrapolation. The fallback is heavier: CosyVoice's reference `run.sh` assumes four GPUs, trains the LLM from the pretrained checkpoint with Adam at 1e-5, 2,500 warmup steps and 2,000 max frames per batch, caps speech tokens at 200 (about 8 seconds at 25 Hz), and "only support[s] llm traning for now" ([cosyvoice2 run.sh](https://github.com/FunAudioLLM/CosyVoice/blob/main/examples/libritts/cosyvoice2/run.sh); [cosyvoice2.yaml](https://github.com/FunAudioLLM/CosyVoice/blob/main/examples/libritts/cosyvoice2/conf/cosyvoice2.yaml)); the 0.5B size makes single-GPU memory a non-issue, but the Turkish evidence rests on Sestek's 2,000-hour multilingual fine-tune whose hyper-parameters are unreported.

Blackwell needs one deliberate software decision. PyTorch 2.7 and later ship cu128 wheels with sm_120 kernels ([PyTorch 2.7 blog](https://pytorch.org/blog/pytorch-2-7/)), but FlashAttention-2 source builds for sm_120 crash nvcc in an issue open since March 2026, official wheels stop at torch 2.8, and FA4 rejects the GB202 die for lack of TMEM ([Dao-AILab #2361](https://github.com/Dao-AILab/flash-attention/issues/2361)); the practical answer is PyTorch SDPA, and a field guide reports roughly 4x from `torch.compile` on sm_120 ([sm120 field guide](https://huggingface.co/datasets/witcheer/sm120-field-guide)). VoxCPM, F5-TTS and CosyVoice do not hard-require flash-attn in their recipes, whereas Orpheus, Unsloth and Qwen3-TTS examples do, which is one more reason the LLM-codec alternatives are a poorer fit for this box.

The corpus needs three transformations before it matches any recipe, and the pipeline already in place (24 kHz mono, −20 LUFS, Whisper-aligned clips gated at CER ≤ 0.20, ECAPA speaker clusters) is a good start. Background music under dialogue must be separated: Emilia-Pipe's Ultimate Vocal Remover step raised mean DNSMOS from 2.52 to 2.88 and subsequent filtering to 3.26, at 2.5 raw hours per minute on eight RTX 4090s ([arXiv 2407.05361](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.05361v3)), and Kur0Keri's VITS kept only clips with DNSMOS ≥ 3.2 and two-pass ASR CER ≤ 0.02 ([HF Kur0Keri](https://huggingface.co/Kur0Keri/turkce-tts-vits)). Clip lengths must be re-cut: VoxCPM2 wants 3–30 s clips with under 0.5 s trailing silence, 16 kHz input for VoxCPM2 training (output stays 48 kHz), and 30–50% of samples paired with a same-speaker `ref_audio` ([VoxCPM fine-tuning guide](https://voxcpm.readthedocs.io/en/latest/finetuning/finetune.html)), whereas the corpus currently spans 1–15 s; the IndexTTS2 community thread found cross-utterance same-speaker pairing necessary to stop prompt bleed ([index-tts #501](https://github.com/index-tts/index-tts/issues/501)), which is where the 0.91-purity speaker clusters earn their keep. Transcripts must be normalised to spoken form with a Turkish normaliser (trnorm or tr-speech) and checked for Whisper's `''` quote artefacts that clash with suffix apostrophes ("Baba'ya") ([GitHub turkish-tts-model](https://github.com/serdarildercaglar/turkish-tts-model)), and casing must map İ to i and I to ı explicitly, since XTTS's own Turkish cleaner never mapped I to ı ([XTTS tokenizer.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/coqui-ai/TTS/dev/TTS/tts/layers/xtts/tokenizer.py)).

The legal position is the most consequential constraint and the least resolved. Turkish copyright law (Law 5846) has a closed list of exceptions with no text-and-data-mining or research-scraping provision, plus a sui generis database right ([law.ungovr.org](https://law.ungovr.org/ai/tr)); performers, phonogram producers and broadcasters hold neighbouring rights ([TBB Dergisi](https://tbbdergisi.barobirlik.org.tr/ViewPDF-eser-sahibinin-haklari-ile-baglantili-haklar-155)); a draft amendment introducing an AI-training licensing mechanism was pending with no confirmed enactment ([Gün + Partners](https://gun.av.tr/insights/updates/key-developments-and-predictions-for-copyright-law-in-turkey)); and the KVKK Board's 2026/921 principle decision lists "ses tınısı" (voice timbre) among biometric special-category data ([CottGroup](https://www.cottgroup.com/en/component/zoo/item/kvkk-ilke-karari-mesai-takibi-amaciyla-biyometrik-veri-islenmesi)). Broadcast drama therefore stacks the programme's copyright, the actors' performance rights, the broadcaster's own right, and the personal-data status of identifiable voices. The Turkish community has managed this with self-imposed gating and open-weights clauses that are not licences from the rights holders, and Freya sidestepped it by training on internal data and a consented voice talent ([arXiv 2607.09530](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09530)). This is a factual summary, not legal advice, but it bears directly on design: use the broadcast corpus to teach Turkish prosody, emotion and non-verbals, ship *designed* voices rather than clones of identifiable actors, and treat weight release as a separate decision requiring counsel.

## Recommended plan: a staged VoxCPM2 LoRA with CosyVoice 3 as fallback

### Model choice and fallback

Primary: VoxCPM2 (2B) with a single shared LoRA of rank 64 on the language model and the diffusion decoder, trained in stages on the Engelsiz corpus, served with VoxCPM2's own voice-design and style-prefix interface so that a user's "config" (emotion, gender, voice description, non-verbal tags) maps onto a Turkish text prefix and inline tags. The choice rests on strong evidence for licence, Turkish support, fine-tuning tooling and community precedent, and on moderate evidence for expressiveness (Ahenk) and naturalness after adaptation (Trendyol's UTMOS 3.82). Fallback: Fun-CosyVoice3-0.5B, trained LLM-first from the released checkpoint with Turkish added as a new language (Qwen BPE already covers the diacritics, language inferred from script as in the Central Asian fine-tune ([HF cosyvoice3-central-asian](https://huggingface.co/Tohirju/cosyvoice3-central-asian))), triggered if the Stage 1 LoRA fails to beat Trendyol-TTS in a blind native-rater CMOS or if naturalness stalls once the corpus exceeds about 200 hours. Because CosyVoice 3 "cannot control acoustic characteristics, such as timbre, through textual instructions" ([arXiv 2505.17589](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.17589v2)), the fallback would still use VoxCPM2 voice design to manufacture reference clips for new voices and clone them in CosyVoice, which keeps the "unique original voice" requirement intact. Keep Chatterbox Multilingual V3 and Trendyol-TTS as zero-effort baselines in every evaluation, and use Higgs TTS 3 and Fish S2 Pro only as listening references, since whether their licences permit using outputs as training data was not verified.

### Data preparation

Separate vocals from the music bed with a UVR/Demucs-class model on the raw episode audio before re-segmenting, then re-run the existing alignment gate, because separation changes the waveform the VAD edges were snapped to. Build a fine-tuning tier stricter than the corpus gate: aligned CER ≤ 0.05 (AKIL used ≤ 0.10, alperiox ≤ 0.05, Kur0Keri ≤ 0.02), DNSMOS ≥ 3.2 after separation, clips 3–20 s with trailing silence under 0.5 s, loudness −20 to −23 LUFS, and a separate "short-utterance" tier of 1–3 s clips held back for a later coverage pass, since short inputs are the hardest setting for every Turkish system measured (Sestek, Antalia, mramazan) and FreyaTTS fixed exactly this with a dedicated short-utterance fine-tune ([arXiv 2607.09530](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09530)). Resample a 16 kHz copy for VoxCPM2 training and keep the 24 kHz masters. Normalise transcripts to spoken form with trnorm or tr-speech, fix İ/I casing, strip Whisper quote artefacts, and write the VoxCPM JSONL with `dataset_id` = speaker cluster and `ref_audio` drawn from a *different* utterance of the same cluster for 30–50% of rows. Write every style instruction in Turkish, not English, because English instructions induced "c" misreads in VoxCPM2 LoRAs ([GitHub ahenk-turkish-tts](https://github.com/ozguradmin/ahenk-turkish-tts)). Mix in licence-clean real speech (Common Voice 25.0, FLEURS-tr, Antalia's 5 h) at 10–20% as neutral replay to counter the forgetting the VoxCPM FAQ warns about, and keep the audiobook and Kiraat scrapes out unless their gating terms (mandatory permissive weight release) are acceptable.

### Manufacturing emotion, gender and non-verbal labels

Run emotion2vec+ large (9-class posterior plus embedding) and EmpathicInsight-Voice (valence, arousal, 40 intensities) on every clean clip and keep a coarse label only where both agree on angry, sad, happy or neutral above a confidence threshold; everything else is "unlabelled" and still trains the prosody stage without a style prefix. Run the laughter detector and a BEATs-style sound-event tagger at frame level to find laughs, sighs, breaths and cries inside clips, and insert Turkish tags (`[gülme]`, `[iç çekme]`, `[ağlama]`) at the time-aligned position in the transcript, exactly as the Elise and NonverbalTTS transcripts do in English. Derive gender per speaker cluster from pitch statistics plus a gender classifier and add it to the style prefix. Before any label is used, have two or three native raters label 300–500 clips sampled across the automatic classes and compute agreement; if the coarse-class agreement is below what TurEV-DB's corpus-specific CNN achieved (about 76% on four emotions ([TurEV-DB](https://aclanthology.org/2020.sltu-1.52.pdf))), raise the thresholds rather than train on noise, because "a few hundred mislabeled laughs will teach the wrong token". Target Elise-scale counts of at least 300 verified instances per non-verbal tag and 20–150 hours of coarse-emotion-labelled speech, which the drama-heavy catalogue should supply once the whole-catalogue run finishes. Request access to Basis Global Emotions (6.12 h Turkish with crying and laughing) strictly as a calibration and held-out test set.

Treat these as planning guesses, not estimates.

### Evaluation protocol

| Dimension | Measure | Comparison set | Caveat |
|---|---|---|---|
| Intelligibility | Band-matched Whisper-large-v3 WER/CER on Freya-TR-Eval with the published scripts; TR-Zor for numbers and abbreviations; a Turkish-fine-tuned Whisper (BuzzASR) as second judge; always report the recogniser's floor on real speech ([HF BuzzASR/turkish](https://huggingface.co/BuzzASR/turkish)) | PatientDesk re-run table | Saturated near 1%; same normaliser on reference and hypothesis |

| Voice identity | WavLM-SV or ECAPA cosine on the MiniMax-24 Turkish protocol (Common Voice prompts) ([arXiv 2505.07916](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.07916)); first-to-last drift on 60 s passages (Sestek) | OmniVoice 0.851, MiniMax 0.779, ElevenLabs 0.596 on that set ([arXiv 2604.00688](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.00688)) | Designed voices need a consistency metric, not a similarity-to-target metric |

| Robustness | Short utterances, "mı"-less questions, code-switched brand names, 300-character inputs | Sestek short-input protocol; TR-Zor categories | Long inputs under-budget duration in several Turkish models |

Publish the blind A/B audio in the existing Turkish TTS Arena format ([HF Space EmreAkgul](https://huggingface.co/spaces/EmreAkgul/turkish-tts-arena)) so that "beats every available Turkish TTS" is a claim others can test rather than a self-report.

### Licensing caveats

VoxCPM2 is Apache-2.0 and its LoRA derivatives inherit that freedom, as Ahenk (CC BY 4.0) and Trendyol (MIT tag) show, but Trendyol's card notes its private dataset remains "subject to the dataset owner's policies" ([HF Trendyol-TTS](https://huggingface.co/Trendyol/Trendyol-TTS)); the training data, not the base, is what constrains the derivative. Chatterbox embeds a PerTh watermark in every output, which is a product consideration if it is ever used as a component ([HF chatterbox README](https://huggingface.co/ResembleAI/chatterbox/blob/main/README.md)). Higgs TTS 3, Fish S2 Pro, Drama 3, Breeze TTS 2, Voxtral, OmniVoice, XTTS-v2, MMS-TTS and the F5 Turkish fine-tune are all non-commercial or ambiguous and must not become ingredients; OmniVoice and Spark-TTS both downgraded their weight licences because of Emilia-style training-data terms, which is a warning about what scraped data does to a release ([HF k2-fsa/OmniVoice](https://huggingface.co/k2-fsa/OmniVoice)). For the broadcast corpus itself, no clearance exists; the mitigations are designed voices, no cloning of identifiable presenters or actors (a KVKK biometric question as well as a performers'-rights one), private weights until counsel has reviewed the FSEK position, and a parallel licence-clean track (Common Voice, FLEURS, Antalia, consented voice talent) that can carry a public release if the broadcast-trained weights cannot.

## Conclusion

The Turkish TTS field has solved the wrong half of the user's problem. A year of community effort produced models that read numbers, dates and "mı" questions almost perfectly and run on a phone, but it did so by distilling one base model's single designed voices into ever-smaller students, and it never measured whether anything it built sounds angry, sad, amused or alive to a Turkish listener. The Engelsiz corpus is the inverse asset: real actors, real emotion, multi-speaker, aligned to human subtitles, exactly the ingredient the synthetic-data pipelines cannot manufacture. That asymmetry, more than any architecture choice, is the strategic reason this project can beat the field, and it dictates the plan's emphasis on separation quality, label calibration and native-rater evaluation over raw hours or model size.

Two implications follow. First, the deliverable that would move the Turkish field is not a checkpoint but a benchmark: a native-rated expressiveness and non-verbal test set in the Freya-TR-Eval style, because today a model that laughs convincingly in Turkish cannot prove it. Second, the legal posture should be designed in from the start rather than retrofitted: voices that are designed rather than cloned, a licence-clean parallel track, and private weights until the FSEK and KVKK questions are answered, so that the best-sounding Turkish voice model is also one that can ship.
